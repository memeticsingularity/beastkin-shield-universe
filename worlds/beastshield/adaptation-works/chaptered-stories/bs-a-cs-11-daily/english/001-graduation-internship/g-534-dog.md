# Gou Laifu's Day

## Scene-1 Morning Light and Ledger

Gou Laifu smelled the meat buns in his dream.

The scent was too real—the sweet fragrance of flour, the oily richness of meat filling mixed with scallions and ginger, the steam rising fresh from the steamer—so real that saliva dripped from the corner of his mouth, soaking a small patch of his pillow. Then the alarm rang. Not a real alarm, but Huang Biao's calloused foot extending from the upper bunk, kicking his bed frame neither lightly nor heavily, the iron bars humming with vibration.

"Puppy, you're drooling." Huang Biao's voice carried the hoarseness of someone not yet fully awake.

Gou Laifu snapped his eyes open. The meat bun scent vanished, replaced by the complex odor of twelve male beastkin stewing through the night in the dormitory: sour sweat, foot stench, fur grease, and the faintly fermented dough-like mustiness wafting from the pile of unwashed socks in the corner. He smacked his lips, tasting only the overnight bitterness on his tongue.

The sky wasn't fully bright yet, but iron-gray light already seeped through the gaps in the tattered burlap nailed over the windows. He lay for a while, listening to the sounds around him. Niu Dazhuang on the left was turning over heavily, the iron bed frame groaning as if about to fall apart. Bao Su on the opposite lower bunk was probably already awake—feline beastkin slept silently, but Gou Laifu could smell his unique, slightly cool grassy scent moving. In the distance, Xiong Houshi's snores rose and fell in spirals.

Gou Laifu fumbled under his pillow for a small cloth bag, rough fabric cut from an old uniform. He didn't open it, just felt it through the cloth. Inside, hard, was his entire fortune: twenty-seven lubi, all small bills, the largest denomination ten, the smallest one-jiao coins. There was also a repeatedly folded paper with his pencil-stub accounting—income, expenses, dream savings. He touched it once before bed and once upon waking, like some ritual.

Twenty-seven. Still far from his goal. He'd calculated that renting the smallest stall in the most remote corner of town would cost at least fifty lubi per month. Three months deposit plus one month rent—that's two hundred. And that didn't even include the capital for stocking inventory.

He sighed, very softly. Then he carefully tucked the bag back under his pillow, pressing it down with the pillow. Only then did he sit up.

The blanket was standard issue, thin as paper, washed stiff, a color that defied description—some kind of grayish yellow. He folded it quickly into a not-quite-regular square and placed it at the head of the bed. Then he put on the green uniform. The uniform was a bit loose for his medium-to-slim build, the cuffs extending too long, requiring him to roll them up twice. The waist was also baggy, secured with a hemp rope tied in a slip knot at the side. The Beastshield logo on the left chest was washed white, the edge stitching loose.

Dressed, he dragged out his washbasin from under the bed. Enamel, with a small hole in the bottom patched from the inside with tin foil, careful not to hold too much water. The toothbrush bristles leaned every which way, the toothpaste was the cheapest kind from the cafeteria, squeezing out chalk-white with a pungent smell.

Three or four people were already lined up at the faucet. He queued, toes unconsciously scraping the ground. The concrete floor was cold and damp, puddled with water splashed by the previous person washing up. When it was his turn, the water pressure was weak, dripping. He collected half a basin, squatted on the stone step at the doorway to brush his teeth. The toothbrush scraped his gums, a little painful, probably bleeding again. He brushed with eyes closed, the mint sting mixing with the sweet metallic taste of blood.

After washing his face, he splashed the water against the wall base, raising a small cloud of dust. He shook the water droplets from his hands, wiping them on his pants. Just then Bao Su returned with six lunch boxes, strung on hemp rope, clinking.

Breakfast.

## Scene-2 The Friendship of Half an Egg

The lunch boxes were arranged on the central square table, lids removed, the contents as always: rice soup thin enough to serve as a mirror, two grayish steamed buns mixed with bran, a pinch of black pickled vegetable strips.

Gou Laifu collected his portion but didn't eat immediately. He first looked at today's egg—the Wednesday benefit, one per person. The egg wasn't large, the shell bearing traces of chicken droppings, but in his eyes it shone with light. He carefully picked it up, holding it in his palm. The egg was warm, carrying the residual heat of the cafeteria's large pot.

He looked around. Niu Dazhuang was eating his buns with his own chili paste, focused, fine sweat beading on his forehead. Huang Biao had already peeled his egg, stuffing the whole thing into his mouth, cheeks bulging. Bao Su was sipping rice soup in small mouthfuls, his egg set aside, untouched. Xiong Houshi and Xiong Dali were competing to see who could peel faster, Xiong Dali's clumsy fingers leaving eggshell fragments all over the table.

Gou Laifu swallowed. He lowered his head and began peeling his egg. The movements were light, slow, careful not to let any egg white stick to the shell. The shell peeled away, revealing smooth egg white, glowing with soft white luster under the dim light. He broke the egg open; the yolk was cooked just right, not runny, golden.

Half, or the whole thing?

He hesitated for a few seconds. Fingers pinching half an egg, he extended it toward Niu Dazhuang: "Brother Niu, for you."

Niu Dazhuang looked up, glanced at the half egg, then at Gou Laifu. "Eat it yourself," he said, voice low.

"I can't finish it," Gou Laifu said, tail wagging unconsciously in small arcs, "Brother Niu, you do heavy work, eat more."

This wasn't true. He was hungry; one egg wasn't enough to fill a gap between his teeth. But Niu Dazhuang was going to send money today, and was also helping him mail a postcard. That postcard... he'd saved for a long time before buying it at a secondhand stall for two jiao, the address on the back copied over a dozen times from the town bulletin board until it was neat. The recipient was the old beggar who had once given him half a flatbread when he was living on the streets, later gone who knows where. Gou Laifu didn't know if he would receive it, just felt there should be something to hold onto.

Niu Dazhuang looked at Gou Laifu's bright, glistening eyes, was silent a moment, then accepted the half egg. "Thanks," he said, placing it on his bun and biting them together.

Gou Laifu smiled, ears perking up. He ate the remaining half egg in small bites, each one held in his mouth to savor slowly. The egg's fragrance, faint saltiness, and that solid, protein-rich satisfaction. With this bit of satisfaction, he could finish both hard buns and the bland rice soup.

Huang Biao watched from the side, snorting with laughter: "Puppy, you sure know how to play the game."

Gou Laifu ignored him, burying his head in his rice soup. The soup had barely any rice grains, just cloudy water with a hint of scorched pot-bottom flavor. But he drank it seriously, licking the last drops from the bottom of the lunch box.

After eating, everyone washed their lunch boxes. Gou Laifu moved quickly, washing his a few times, then placing it upside down on the windowsill. Then he sidled up to Niu Dazhuang, asking softly: "Brother Niu, the postcard... the address wasn't wrong, right?"

Niu Dazhuang pulled out the folded postcard from inside his shirt, unfolding it to show him: "Is this the place?"

Gou Laifu looked carefully. The address characters were crooked but the strokes were clear. "Yes, that's it," he nodded, then added, "For the stamp money, I'll definitely pay you back next month when I get my pay."

Niu Dazhuang grunted, tucking the postcard back away. Gou Laifu watched it disappear into Niu Dazhuang's thick chest, feeling inexplicably more settled.

The whistle blew.

## Scene-3 The Maze of Scents

Warehouse Area A was Gou Laifu and Bao Su's assigned zone.

Pushing open the heavy iron door, darkness wrapped in familiar scents surged out. Gou Laifu took a deep breath, nose twitching. His canine sense of smell allowed him to distinguish countless layers within this mixed odor: the dry astringency of aged dust, the faintly sweet mustiness of damp wood, the metallic fishiness of rust, and the indescribable chemical scents emanating from various cargo packaging materials. Above these background smells, there were more subtle ones—the ammonia tang of rat urine, the decaying smell of insects caught in spiderwebs in some corner, and even a faint, almost imperceptible sweetness from syrup spilled long ago, now completely dried.

He flicked on his flashlight. The beam cut through the darkness, illuminating cargo boxes stacked to the ceiling ahead. The boxes varied in size and material, like silent giants in the shadows.

"Same as usual?" he asked Bao Su.

Bao Su nodded without speaking. The two walked one after another into the alleyways formed by the cargo piles. Gou Laifu took the lead, the flashlight beam sweeping left and right, checking seals and securing ropes. Bao Su followed two steps behind, footsteps light as a cat's, almost inaudible, but Gou Laifu could smell his cool, calm scent, like a reassurance pill.

Area A contained miscellaneous goods. There were whole cases of work gloves, heavy with canvas smell; mountains of metal parts, emitting the odor of anti-rust oil; and some thick cardboard boxes whose contents were indiscernible, only the blurred writing on the seals hinting at what was inside.

Gou Laifu inspected carefully. He didn't just look, he also used his nose. Did the seal glue have any abnormal scent? Were there any liquid traces on the box surface that shouldn't be there? Had the securing rope knots been tampered with? His nose was more reliable than his eyes.

When they reached the edge of Area B, he suddenly stopped.

"Wait," he whispered, nose inhaling forcefully in one direction.

Bao Su immediately halted, hand moving to the baton at his waist.

Gou Laifu walked toward the scent source. It was a pile of wooden crates in the depths of Area B, stacked more neatly than elsewhere, the seals also newer. He leaned close to one crate, nose almost pressed to the gap between the boards.

"This smell..." he frowned.

"What?" Bao Su asked.

"Can't say for sure," Gou Laifu sniffed again, "A bit sweet, a bit fishy, and... a chemical sting. All mixed together." He stepped back, shining the flashlight on the batch number and seals of these wooden crates. The numbers were consecutive, the seals intact, but the smell was wrong.

Bao Su also came over, crouching to inspect. His eyes reflected faintly in the dim light. "Seals haven't been touched." He observed for a while, then pulled out a nearly transparent thin thread from his small bag—some material, barely thicker than a hair. He carefully tied it to an inconspicuous iron clasp on the wooden crate, the other end to a shelf post nearby, the thread pulled taut.

"Marker," Bao Su explained briefly. "If someone moves it, the thread will break or loosen."

Gou Laifu nodded, writing on his record board: Area B West, wooden crate pile (numbers BT-108 to BT-115), seals intact, but unusual mixed odor detected. Recommend attention.

Finished writing, he looked at Bao Su. The black panther beastkin had already straightened up, expression still calm, but Gou Laifu could sense the slight tension in his muscles, a state of alert.

"What could it be?" Gou Laifu couldn't help asking.

Bao Su shook his head: "Don't know. Don't ask, just record it."

The two continued their patrol. But Gou Laifu couldn't shake that strange smell from his mind. Sweet, fishy, chemical. He'd smelled something similar when he was living on the streets—once at a black market deal, when those people opened crates, this exact sweet-fishy with underlying chemical sting wafted out. Later he heard it was some kind of banned "happy powder."

He shook his head, throwing off these thoughts. Can't think wildly. In Beastshield, the less you know, the longer you live.

The flashlight beam continued cutting through the darkness. The shadows of cargo crates stretched and twisted in the light, like monsters about to pounce at any moment. Gou Laifu's ears stood upright, capturing every sound beyond their footsteps. In the distance was a dripping sound, very regular, probably a leaking pipe somewhere. Further away, there seemed to be an extremely faint, metallic creaking sound, but it was indistinct.

His tail swayed slightly behind him unconsciously, a habit when he was nervous. Muscles beneath his fur tensed, ready to respond to sudden situations. But nothing happened. Only darkness, silence, and endless cargo crates.

When they reached a relatively open area, Bao Su suddenly spoke: "You want to open a shop?"

Gou Laifu was startled, not expecting Bao Su to initiate conversation. "Yeah," he nodded, "Selling groceries, or snacks. Anything works."

"Takes a lot of money," Bao Su said, voice calm, indistinguishable whether it was a statement or a question.

"I know," Gou Laifu said, tail drooping slightly, "I'm saving. If I can save twenty a month, that's two hundred forty in a year. Borrow some more, maybe it'll be enough."

He said it lightly, but he knew clearly in his heart. One hundred lubi per month, food and clothing basically cost nothing, but there were always unexpected expenses—medicine when sick, compensation for damaged tools, occasional contributions for colleagues' weddings and funerals. Saving twenty was already the limit. Two hundred forty a year, still far from his goal. And who would lend him money?

"Not easy," Bao Su said, pausing, "But better than staying here."

Gou Laifu looked at Bao Su in surprise. The black panther beastkin's profile was hidden in shadow, expression unreadable. This was the first time Bao Su had said such things to him.

"Brother Su, you... you've never thought about leaving?" Gou Laifu asked carefully.

Bao Su was silent for a long time. So long that Gou Laifu thought he wouldn't answer. Just as Gou Laifu was about to change the subject, Bao Su spoke: "Thought about it. But some debts, not yet repaid, can't leave."

Debts? What debts? Gou Laifu wanted to ask, but Bao Su had already stepped forward, ending the conversation.

Gou Laifu followed, mind turning over Bao Su's words. Debts? Money debts? Favor debts? Or... something else? He recalled some rumors about Bao Su—saying he wasn't an ordinary beastkin before, his skills were extraordinarily good, like he'd received professional training. Why had he ended up here as a warehouse guard? No one knew.

The flashlight beam continued forward. The darkness seemed to have no end.

## Scene-4 The Secret of Noon Break

Two hours for noon break.

Gou Laifu didn't return to the dormitory to sleep like the others. He waited until everyone was pretty much lying down, then tiptoed out of the dormitory, crossed the front courtyard, and circled around to the abandoned tool shed behind the warehouse.

The lock on the tool shed door had long been broken, secured only with a wire twist. He skillfully unwound the wire, pushed the door open, and immediately closed it behind him.

Inside was cooler than outside, but also more stuffy. The air was thick with dust, rust, and the musty smell of old paper dampened by moisture. Light filtered in through gaps in the door panels and a small broken window high above, forming several dim yellow light columns with dust dancing within them.

Gou Laifu walked to the corner, moved aside several stacked empty wooden crates, revealing a small space covered with oilcloth at the base of the wall behind. He lifted the oilcloth, revealing his "treasures": several old books and magazines he'd scavenged, a chipped clay pot, and his most precious "Shop Opening Guide."

He picked up the "Shop Opening Guide," its pages already blackened and curled from repeated flipping. He sat beside the light column, carefully opening it. He couldn't recognize all the characters in the book, but by guessing and looking at some simple diagrams, he could understand the general idea.

Today he flipped to the "Cost Accounting" chapter. It had tables drawn, listing various expenses: rent, utilities, inventory costs, labor... The numbers were like a heavenly book to him, but he looked very seriously, finger pointing at the characters, moving line by line, lips moving soundlessly.

"Monthly rent... fifty... three months deposit plus one month rent..." he muttered softly, pulling out the small cloth bag from inside his shirt, pouring out the money. Twenty-seven lubi, spread on the dirty ground. He then picked up a small twig from nearby, scratching calculations on the ground.

Twenty-seven. Twenty-three short of fifty. One hundred seventy-three short of two hundred.

He sighed, throwing the twig aside. Then from the book pages he carefully pulled out a folded paper. It was his own sketch of his "dream shop"—drawn in pencil, lines crooked, but showing a small storefront with a counter, shelves, and even a signboard at the door with "Laifu Groceries" written on it, childish handwriting.

He looked at that drawing for a long time. His tail wagged unconsciously, small amplitude but cheerful.

Footsteps sounded extremely lightly outside the door.

Gou Laifu immediately became alert, ears perking up, tail stopping. He quickly stuffed the sketch back into the book, closed it, together with the money covered by the oilcloth, then moved the empty crates back to their original positions. Then he held his breath, pressing against the door.

The footsteps paused outside for a moment. Then, the door was pushed open.

It was Bao Su.

Gou Laifu breathed a sigh of relief, but immediately tensed again—how did Bao Su find this place?

"Knew you'd be here," Bao Su said, voice calm. He walked in, casually closing the door. The tool shed grew darker, only that beam from the broken window remained.

"Brother Su, you..." Gou Laifu didn't know what to say.

Bao Su didn't explain, just walked to where he'd been sitting, crouched down, and felt the ground with his hand—there were the scratch marks Gou Laifu had made with the twig. Then he looked up, toward the corner where Gou Laifu hid his things.

"This place isn't safe," Bao Su said. "Lao Qu told me yesterday he saw the foreman wandering around here."

Gou Laifu's heart tightened. Lao Qu was that old hedgehog beastkin in the dormitory, sharp-nosed and well-informed. "What's the foreman doing here?"

"Don't know," Bao Su stood up, "But be careful. Your things, better find another place."

Gou Laifu nodded, heart in turmoil. He'd spent a long time finding this relatively hidden place. Another place? Where? The dormitory was crowded with prying eyes, nothing could be hidden there.

Bao Su seemed to read his difficulty. "We'll talk tonight," he said, "Go back first, noon break is almost over."

The two left the tool shed one after another. Gou Laifu rewound the wire, checked again, confirming it looked normal. The sunlight was fierce, making him squint. The warehouse's massive shadow fell on the ground, like a crouching giant beast.

On the way back to the dormitory, Bao Su suddenly said: "About the money, don't rush. I know someone who runs short-distance transport, occasionally needs help unloading private cargo, pays cash, not much, but better than nothing."

Gou Laifu turned his head sharply to look at Bao Su, eyes wide: "Really?"

"Yeah," Bao Su nodded, "But high risk. If caught, it's not as simple as getting fired."

Gou Laifu fell silent. He needed money, but he was also afraid. He knew Beastshield's rules well—taking private jobs was a major taboo. But if one time could earn ten or twenty...

"I'll think about it," he said.

Bao Su said nothing more.

When they returned to the dormitory, the noon break ending whistle was just sounding. The dormitory was in chaos, everyone rubbing their eyes and getting up, cursing as they put on shoes. Niu Dazhuang was already fully dressed, tucking that lunch box containing money back under the bed. Huang Biao was yawning, the scar on his face looking particularly ferocious in the afternoon light.

Gou Laifu walked to his own bed, pretending to tidy his bedding, hand reaching under the pillow to feel. The cloth bag was still there. He breathed a sigh of relief.

There was still work in the afternoon. He took a deep breath, pushing the thoughts of the shop, the private job Bao Su mentioned, and whether the foreman had discovered something, all down to the bottom of his heart.

Life had to go on, work had to be done.

## Scene-5 Twilight Delivery

The afternoon work was supervising loading and receiving new cargo.

Gou Laifu and Bao Su were assigned to coordinate with the truck driver. The driver was a hyena beastkin, face full of impatience, slapping the delivery slip into Gou Laifu's hand with considerable force.

"Hurry up, I'm running late," the driver said, cigarette dangling from his mouth, smoke blowing into Gou Laifu's face.

Gou Laifu suppressed a cough, carefully checking the slip. Areas D-22 to D-25, twenty cases of hardware parts. He nodded to Bao Su, and the two walked into the warehouse.

The cases to be picked up had already been moved out by Niu Dazhuang and the others, stacked in the front courtyard. Gou Laifu's task was to check each case's seal and number, ensure they matched the slip, then do one more inspection before loading.

He worked very seriously. For each case he crouched down to look closely, flashlight shining on the seal edges, checking for damage or signs of secondary adhesion. The numbers were written in chalk on the side of the cases, some blurred, so he'd wipe them with his sleeve to confirm.

Bao Su supervised the loading process nearby. The two temporary workers the driver brought moved cases onto the truck bed, Bao Su's eyes tracking every movement, ensuring the cases were placed steadily, wouldn't tip or collide during transport.

When they reached the tenth case, Gou Laifu noticed the seal edge was slightly curled. He leaned in to look, gently touching it with his hand. The glue seemed a bit uneven, but overall still secure. He hesitated, then noted on his record board: Case D-24, seal edge slightly curled, no damage seen.

"Pup, what's taking so long?" the driver shouted from not far away.

"Almost done!" Gou Laifu replied, speeding up.

All loaded, the driver signed the paperwork, jumped into the cab. The truck started, spewing a puff of black smoke, slowly driving out the gate.

Gou Laifu watched the truck disappear into the dust, only then relaxing. Turning back, he saw the new cargo had already been unloaded nearby—another pile of cases, varying sizes, seals brand new.

"Inventory check," Bao Su said.

The two began counting the new cargo. Case quantities, numbers, exterior condition... Gou Laifu recorded each item. He noticed these cases had particularly new seals, the stamps bright red, like they'd just been applied. But strangely, several case corners had slight collision marks, the wooden case paint chipped.

"These have dents," he pointed out to Bao Su.

Bao Su crouched to inspect, feeling the dented areas with his hand. "Record it. Probably from loading."

Gou Laifu recorded it. Inventory complete, the two moved the new cargo into the designated warehouse area. The cases weren't heavy, but there were many, several trips back and forth, Gou Laifu's back already sweating. The green uniform stuck to his skin, sticky and uncomfortable.

After moving the last case, the sun had already begun to set in the west. Sunset clouds dyed the sky orange-red, the warehouse's tin roof reflecting warm light, but Gou Laifu knew that warmth was false. Once the sun set, it would get cold again.

In the front courtyard, the day shift people gathered, waiting for shift change. Gou Laifu handed his record board to the night shift foreman—still that wild boar beastkin, yawning.

"Recorded the wooden crate odor in Area B?" the boar foreman glanced at the record board.

"Recorded," Gou Laifu said.

"Mm." The foreman didn't ask further, waving his hand, "Alright, scram."

Shift change complete. Gou Laifu returned to the group, seeing Niu Dazhuang just tucking that lunch box containing money back under the bed. The postcard should have been sent by now, right? Gou Laifu thought, feeling a little empty inside. That two-jiao stamp money, he must pay back next month.

Huang Biao walked over, patting Gou Laifu's shoulder with considerable force. "Pup, didn't cause trouble today?"

"No," Gou Laifu said.

"Good then," Huang Biao grinned, the scar twisting, "In this world, being honest lets you live a few more days."

Gou Laifu didn't know how to respond. Huang Biao had already turned and walked away, his figure in the gradually darkening sky looking somewhat hunched.

The six dispersed in their own directions. Gou Laifu was the last to leave the front courtyard. He looked back at the warehouse. The main gate was being slowly closed by the night shift people, the iron doors meeting with a heavy, prolonged sound, like a sigh.

Then, the lock clicked shut.

## Scene-6 The Abacus Under the Oil Lamp

Dinner was the same as always. Thin porridge, hard steamed buns, pickled vegetables.

Gou Laifu ate quickly. He was hungry; he'd only eaten half an egg and two buns at noon, long since digested. The porridge was too thin, he drank it in a few gulps, the buns with pickled vegetables, chewing until his jaw ached.

After eating, he helped the cafeteria uncle collect lunch boxes, earning a mumbled "thanks." Then he returned to the dormitory, where the oil lamp was already lit.

The dormitory was filled with the black smoke of burning oil lamps and the thick body odor of gathered male beastkin. Gou Laifu walked to his bed, not lying down immediately. He hesitated, then fumbled under his pillow for the small cloth bag, stealing a glance at Bao Su.

Bao Su sat on his own bunk, wiping his always-clean shoes with a cloth. Sensing Gou Laifu's gaze, he looked up and nodded slightly.

Gou Laifu understood. He tucked the cloth bag into his shirt, pretending to go to the toilet, and slipped out of the dormitory.

The night was already thick. No moon, only a few stars coldly nailed to the sky. The warehouse's massive outline in the darkness was like a sleeping giant beast, only a few safety lights glowing in the distance, their faint halos blurred in the night mist.

Gou Laifu circled to the side of the warehouse, where there was a place piled with discarded packaging materials. Cardboard boxes, wooden strips, torn burlap bags stacked high, forming a patch of chaotic shadows. He waited in the shadows for a while, until his eyes adapted to the darkness.

Bao Su came, footsteps light as a ghost.

"Here," Bao Su said in a low voice, leading Gou Laifu to the deepest part of the scrap pile. There was a gap, barely enough for two people to crouch. Bao Su moved aside several empty cardboard boxes, revealing behind them a rusted metal cabinet—some kind of file cabinet discarded who knows when, the door already warped.

"Here," Bao Su said, forcefully pulling open the warped cabinet door. Inside was empty, thick with accumulated dust. "Normally no one comes. You can put your things here, cover them with cardboard boxes."

Gou Laifu looked around. It was indeed hidden here, the scrap pile emitting a strong musty and chemical residue smell, normal people wouldn't want to approach. He took out the small cloth bag from inside his shirt, hesitated, then pulled out the "Shop Opening Guide" and that sketch.

"Just put it here?" He was a bit uneasy.

"Safer than the tool shed," Bao Su said. "The tool shed is too conspicuous, and the foreman has already noticed."

Gou Laifu thought about it, then nodded. He placed his things at the very back of the metal cabinet, then blocked the cabinet door with several empty cardboard boxes. After finishing, he patted the dust from his hands, but his heart felt even emptier. The things were hidden, but his dream was still so distant.

"Brother Su," he asked in a small voice, "That private job you mentioned... does it really pay cash?"

Bao Su was silent in the darkness for a few seconds. "Five per job. Unload one truck, midnight work, two hours to finish."

Five. Gou Laifu calculated quickly in his heart. Five was equivalent to what he could save in half a month. If he could do it twice a month, that's ten. That way, his saving speed could double.

But the risk? What happens if caught? Fired? Beaten? Or worse?

"I... I'll think about it," he said, voice trembling slightly.

Bao Su didn't pressure him. "Tell me when you've decided," he said. "But remember, once you decide to do it, you can't back out. Those people... not to be trifled with."

Gou Laifu nodded. He knew who "those people" were—the local bosses in town, with千丝万缕 connections to certain Beastshield personnel, specializing in shady business. Working for them, money came fast, but you might not come back.

The two crouched in silence in the darkness. In the distance came wild dog barks, one, two, then silence. Night wind swept over the warehouse tin roof, making a wailing sound.

"Let's go back," Bao Su stood up, "Don't let anyone discover us."

Gou Laifu followed, standing up, legs a bit numb. The two slipped back to the dormitory one after another. The oil lamp was still lit, most people in the room already lying down. Niu Dazhuang leaned against the head of the bed, reading a letter by lamplight—probably from home, he read very slowly, very carefully. Huang Biao was already asleep, snores not loud but regular. Xiong Houshi and Xiong Dali were murmuring something in the corner, voices indistinct.

Gou Laifu tiptoed to his own bed, took off his shoes, lay down. The bed board was hard, the blanket thin, but he was used to it. He lay with eyes open, watching the swaying, uncertain shadows cast by the oil lamp on the ceiling.

Five. Risk. Opening a shop. Two hundred lubi. These thoughts spun around in his mind.

He turned over, facing the wall. A patch of wall plaster had peeled away, revealing the yellow mud and straw mixed filler inside. He reached out to touch it, rough, prickly.

If he could open a shop... he imagined the scene. A small storefront, shelves stocked with cigarettes, alcohol, candy, daily necessities. He stood behind the counter, someone came in, he smiled and said "Welcome." After closing at night, he counted the day's earnings, not much, but all his own. He could buy meat to eat, could send some money to that old beggar who'd taken him in, could even... find a mate?

Thinking about it, the corner of his mouth curved up unconsciously. His tail wagged gently under the thin blanket.

Then he remembered Bao Su's words: those people are not to be trifled with.

The smile disappeared.

He closed his eyes, forcing himself to sleep. Tomorrow he had to get up early, patrol, face those crates whose contents he didn't know, smell those strange odors, worry about whether the foreman had discovered something.

Life was like this. Day after day of repetition, hope slim, but he had to hold onto that bit of hope to keep going.

He counted sheep. One sheep, two sheep... counting to who knows how many, consciousness began to blur.

Before completely falling asleep, his last thought was: that postcard, where is it now? That old beggar, is he still alive? Will he remember me?

No answers.

Only darkness, and the distant, never-ending wild dog barks.

(The End of Gou Laifu's Day)
