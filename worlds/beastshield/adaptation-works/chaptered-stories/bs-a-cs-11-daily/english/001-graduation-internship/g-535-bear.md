# Xiong Houshi's Day

## Scene-1 Warm and Fuzzy Awakening

Heat. Heat was the first thing he felt.

Xiong Houshi woke in a bundle of warm, fuzzy heat, like being wrapped in a quilt freshly sun-dried. His consciousness slowly floated up from very deep, like a piece of wood soaked for ages finally surfacing. Touch returned first—the hardness of his back against the bed board, the slight pressure of his belly on the thin mattress, and the warmth covering his entire body from the thick, dense layer of white fur.

Polar bear beastkin had a basal body temperature one and a half degrees higher than most beastkin, plus a thick fat layer and dense double coat of fur. He was like a self-heating plush bundle, almost never cold even in winter. Here in the dormitory crowded with twelve male beastkin, the temperature around his bunk was noticeably higher than elsewhere, forming a small warm zone.

He opened his eyes. Dark, but not too dark for him. Polar bears could see in polar nights; his eyes adapted well to low light. He could see the yellow water stains on the ceiling from rain leaks, like a blurry map. He could see the green uniforms hanging on the opposite bunk, like lowered flags in the darkness. He could see the white fur on his own chest, rising and falling with his breath, like snow-covered hills.

He didn't move immediately. First he lay there, enjoying this moment of peace after waking. This was the most comfortable time of his day—well-rested, body full of energy, the day's labor not yet begun. He took a deep breath. The air held that familiar dormitory smell: sour sweat, foot odor, fur grease, overnight farts, low-quality tobacco residue, and that fishy-muttony reek of various hair and sebum fermenting in the stuffy heat. But strangely, he didn't hate this smell. It represented "here," represented "companions," represented "safety." In the wild, a lone bear was very dangerous. Here, though crowded, though smelly, with companions breathing, snoring, turning over around him, he felt at ease.

His ears turned slightly, collecting sounds. From the lower bunk on the left came Niu Dazhuang's heavy breathing, a slight hum in his nasal cavity with each inhale, like an old blower. On the upper right was Huang Biao, sleeping restlessly, grinding his teeth, the fine grinding sound like a rat gnawing wood. Diagonally across, Gou Laifu was talking in his sleep, indistinct, but you could make out him calling "Ma..." Further away, Xiong Dali was snoring, sound deep and even, like a well-running engine. Bao Su's side was completely silent—the black panther beastkin slept as quietly as a shadow, but Xiong Houshi could smell his unique, slightly cool grassy scent and knew he was there.

Everything was fine. Everyone was here. Safe.

Xiong Houshi exhaled contentedly, white mist briefly lingering in the cold air before dissipating. He began slowly moving his body, starting from his toes. Ten stubby toes slowly spread and curled inside his socks, feeling the cotton fibers rubbing against the thick pads on his soles. Then his calf muscles, gently tensing and relaxing, feeling the gastrocnemius like two full pebbles rolling under the skin. Thighs, buttocks, abdomen, chest, arms, fingers, finally neck and head. He was like doing a silent radio calisthenics routine, letting blood flow through his entire body again, letting stiff joints relubricate.

After all this, he finally sat up. Not fast, even somewhat clumsy—his massive frame needed time to adjust its center of gravity. He first braced himself on the bed board with thick arms, biceps and triceps tensing simultaneously, pink skin beneath the white fur stretched shiny by the muscles. Then his waist exerted force, thick back muscles bulging, straining the green uniform taut. Finally his legs pushed, and he sat straight up.

The iron bed frame groaned in pain, but less than when Niu Dazhuang got up—Xiong Houshi's weight was also close to three hundred jin, but his rising motion was more even, not like Niu Dazhuang's sudden push. He sat on the bed edge, two thick legs hanging down, feet still a palm's height from the floor. His feet were large, thick, soles broad, arches nearly flat, typical of ground-walking animals.

He looked down at his belly. The white fur-covered tummy bulged roundly, deforming the green uniform pants' elastic band. But he knew beneath this fat layer was solid abdominal muscle—not the kind with defined blocks and clear lines, but one thick slab, like a shield, protecting the internal organs. He patted his belly with his hand, producing dull thumping sounds, the feel solid and elastic, like patting a waterskin filled to capacity.

Time to get up. He bent down, dragging out his washbasin from under the bed. It was extra-large, the small basins ordinary beastkin used like toys to him. Enamel, white, with "Beastshield" printed on the bottom, the edges chipped in several places revealing black iron underneath. The toothbrush was also extra-large, the brush head twice the size of normal ones, bristles hard as boar hair. The toothpaste was the cheapest kind from the cafeteria, squeezing out chalk-white with a pungent smell, but one tube lasted him a week.

He stood up with the basin, his massive frame nearly blocking the aisle. He had to turn sideways to squeeze through. Passing Niu Dazhuang's bunk, he carefully lifted his feet to avoid kicking Niu Dazhuang's boots placed beside the bed. Passing Huang Biao's lower bunk, he held his breath—Huang Biao had morning temper, would curse if woken.

Three or four people were already lined up at the faucet. Xiong Houshi obediently queued at the end. He was tall enough to see over the heads of those in front to the faucet. The water was very small, dripping, like the urine of a dying patient. Those in front took a long time, each washing for ages. Xiong Houshi wasn't impatient, just stood waiting. He liked queuing time—could space out, think about nothing, watch the sky gradually brighten.

Light seeping through the gaps in the tattered burlap nailed over the windows changed from iron-gray to turbid grayish-white. Dawn. Xiong Houshi could hear the creak of the iron door opening from the warehouse in the distance, and early bird calls, far away, like something from another world.

When it was his turn, the water was even smaller, almost a thin thread. He collected half a basin—the basin was too large, he couldn't carry it if full. Then he squatted on the stone step at the doorway to brush his teeth. The toothbrush moved back and forth in his mouth, hard bristles scraping his gums, a bit painful, but he was used to it. Polar bear teeth were sturdy, could crush seal bones, this little irritation was nothing. Toothpaste foam dissolved in his mouth, mint sting mixing with chemical fragrance's pungency, he brushed with eyes closed, very seriously, every tooth attended to.

After brushing, he cupped water to wash his face. The water was ice-cold, stabbing into his face, making him hiss involuntarily. But he liked this feeling—awake, refreshing. He washed very carelessly, palms rubbing haphazardly across his face a few circles, water flowing down his neck into his collar, making him shudder. Finished, he splashed the water against the wall base, raising a large cloud of dust.

Just then Bao Su returned with six lunch boxes, strung on hemp rope, clinking.

Breakfast time. Xiong Houshi's stomach rumbled appropriately.

## Scene-2 The Ritual of Breakfast

Lunch boxes arranged on the central square table, lids removed, the contents unchanged for ten years: rice soup thin enough to serve as a mirror, two grayish steamed buns mixed with bran, a pinch of black pickled vegetable strips. But today was different—today was Wednesday, and each person also had a boiled egg.

Xiong Houshi's eyes lit up when he saw the eggs. Not a metaphor, they actually lit up—polar bear eyes reflected faintly when excited, like two obsidian stones. He stared at that egg, Adam's apple rolling, swallowing saliva.

But he didn't take it immediately. He first collected his portion, carried his lunch box to his spot—the lower bunk by the window, where the light was good, allowing him to eat while looking outside. He sat down, lunch box resting steadily on his thick thighs. Then he arranged his eating order: first buns and pickled vegetables, then rice soup, finally the egg. The egg he would save for last, as the climax and reward of this meager breakfast.

He picked up a bun. The bun was cold, hard as a rock, looking small in his thick hands. He broke it open, the inside even grayer, visible bran granules like sand mixed in. But he didn't mind. Polar bear digestive systems were powerful, could digest rotten meat, bones, even the fat layer of seals. What was this little bran. He stuffed half the bun into his mouth, chewing. Cheeks bulging like two ping pong balls stuffed inside. Masseter muscles on both sides of his face bulged, contracting and releasing with the chewing motion.

The bun had little flavor, just the bland sweetness of starch and the roughness of bran. He ate it with the pickled vegetables. The pickled vegetable strips were black, looking like dried radish, but salty to the point of bitterness, making him frown. But he still ate, one bite of bun, one strip of pickled vegetable, chewing slowly, swallowing slowly.

Niu Dazhuang sat across from him, eating his buns with his own chili paste. The chili paste's fragrance drifted over, spicy with the richness of fermented black beans. Xiong Houshi's nose twitched, eyes involuntarily glancing over. Niu Dazhuang noticed, looked up at him, smiled honestly: "Houshi, want some dip?"

Xiong Houshi hesitated. He knew Niu Dazhuang's chili paste was precious, homemade by his mate and sent through someone. But he really couldn't resist the fragrance. He extended a stubby finger, carefully dipping just a tiny bit, really just fingernail-sized, then smeared it on the heart of his bun.

The chili paste was very spicy, but fragrant. It was the taste of hometown sun-dried red peppers and fermented black beans pounded together then oil-fried. Just that little bit, and the whole bun came alive. Xiong Houshi squinted his eyes, chewing carefully, letting the spiciness and aroma fully diffuse in his mouth. He hummed contentedly, tail patting the bed board lightly—polar bear tails were very short, hidden in thick rump fur, but they still wagged unconsciously when excited.

"Good, right?" Niu Dazhuang asked, voice low and gentle.

"Yeah!" Xiong Houshi nodded vigorously, pink nose tip beneath the white fur moist and glistening, "Really good! Thanks Brother Niu!"

"No need for thanks," Niu Dazhuang said, continuing to eat his own.

Huang Biao snorted with laughter nearby: "Silly big guy, a little chili paste and you're this happy."

Xiong Houshi ignored him. He was used to Huang Biao's sarcasm, knew retorting would only invite more mockery. He buried his head and continued eating, finishing both buns and pickled vegetables, then lifted his lunch box to drink the rice soup. He drank noisily, sound loud, like he was drinking some delicacy.

Finally, time to eat the egg.

He carefully picked up the egg, holding it in his palm. The egg was warm, carrying the residual heat of the cafeteria's large pot, the shell bearing traces of dried chicken droppings and straw scraps. He peeled it carefully, movements clumsy but earnest. Thick fingers pinching the small eggshell, peeling bit by bit, afraid of breaking the egg white. Finished peeling, the whole egg smooth and intact, egg white glowing with soft white luster in the morning light, like a huge pearl.

He looked at the egg for several seconds, like performing some ritual. Then he opened his mouth, putting the whole egg inside. The egg wasn't large, just right for his big mouth. He closed his mouth, began chewing. Egg white smooth and tender, yolk dense and creamy, with faint fishy and salty taste. He held it like this, chewing slowly, letting the egg fragrance fill his entire mouth, slide down his esophagus, warm his whole stomach.

This was one of the moments he most looked forward to each week. The weekly Wednesday egg was a bright spot in his monotonous life, a bit of tangible comfort. He ate very slowly, very cherishingly, chewing more than twenty times per bite, letting his taste buds fully enjoy.

Gou Laifu came over, holding half an egg in his hand: "Brother Houshi, you eat so deliciously."

Xiong Houshi turned to look at him, mouth still containing egg, speaking indistinctly: "Yeah! Good!"

"Want half my egg?" Gou Laifu said, eyes bright and glistening.

Xiong Houshi shook his head, swallowing the egg in his mouth with effort: "No need, you eat yourself. You're still growing."

"I'm not small anymore," Gou Laifu muttered, but still took back the half egg, eating it in small bites.

Bao Su was eating quietly not far away. He peeled eggs with extreme patience, egg white unbroken, then put the whole thing in his mouth, chewing slowly. Eating buns was also one chopstickful at a time, silently sending to his mouth. Xiong Houshi watched Bao Su, feeling a bit envious—Bao Su did everything so elegantly, so methodically, unlike himself, clumsy, eating sloppily.

Xiong Dali came over with his lunch box, plopping down beside Xiong Houshi, the bed board groaning in pain. Xiong Dali was even bulkier than Xiong Houshi, a brown bear beastkin, deep brown fur, larger muscle blocks, thicker fat layer. The egg in his lunch box was already finished, now gnawing on his third bun.

"Houshi, why do you eat so slow?" Xiong Dali said indistinctly, bun crumbs at the corner of his mouth.

"I... I chew carefully and swallow slowly," Xiong Houshi said, "Ma said, chewing carefully is good for health."

"Oh." Xiong Dali responded, continuing to bury his head in eating.

The two bear beastkin sat together, like two fuzzy little mountains, pressing the bed deeply凹陷. They emitted similar body scents—the rich bear-class-specific odor, mixed with sweat and faint honey-like sweetness. This was their race's unique pheromone, used in the wild for marking territory and identifying kin. Here, this smell made them feel close.

After eating, everyone washed their lunch boxes. Xiong Houshi moved slowly, by the time he reached the faucet the water was already very small, almost dripping out. He collected some water, rubbing the inside and outside of the lunch box with his fingers. The aluminum lunch box looked small in his thick hands, like a toy. Finished washing, he shook off the water, placing the lunch box upside down on the windowsill to dry. The cement windowsill surface was pressed slightly凹陷 by the weight of his lunch box.

The whistle blew. Sharp and short, like a blade slicing through the thin morning air.

Time to work.

## Scene-3 The Touch of Weight

Warehouse Area B was Xiong Houshi and Xiong Dali's assigned zone.

Pushing open the heavy iron door, darkness wrapped in familiar scents surged out. Xiong Houshi took a deep breath, nose twitching. His sense of smell wasn't as keen as canines, but better than most beastkin. He could distinguish the main components of this mixed odor: the dry astringency of aged dust, the faintly sweet mustiness of damp wood, the metallic fishiness of rust, and the indescribable chemical scents from various cargo packaging materials. Above these background smells, there were more subtle ones—the ammonia tang of rat urine, the decaying smell of insects caught in spiderwebs in some corner.

He flicked on his flashlight. The beam stabbed into the darkness, illuminating cargo boxes stacked to the ceiling ahead. The boxes were larger than Area A's, heavier, mostly metal cases and thick wooden crates, stacked neatly, like a group of silent steel giants.

"Same as usual?" Xiong Dali asked, voice echoing in the emptiness.

"Yeah." Xiong Houshi nodded. The two walked one after another into the alleyways formed by the cargo piles. Xiong Houshi took the lead, flashlight beam sweeping left and right. His inspection method differed from Bao Su's—Bao Su relied on fingertip touch and keen smell, he relied on eyes and strength.

He would stop, push a box with his hand, feeling the weight and stability. If the box wobbled, it meant the securing rope was loose and needed rebinding. He would wrap thick arms around the case, testing whether the seal was secure. If the seal edge could be pried up a bit by his thick fingers, it meant it might have been tampered with.

Reaching a batch of wooden crates, he stopped.

"Dali, come here," he beckoned.

Xiong Dali came over, and the two together lifted a case from the middle layer of the cargo pile. The case was very heavy, at least two hundred jin. Xiong Houshi held one end, Xiong Dali the other, both exerting force simultaneously, muscles tensing, skin beneath white and brown fur reddening, veins bulging on foreheads and arms. The case left the ground, placed steadily in the middle of the alleyway.

Xiong Houshi crouched down, flashlight shining on the bottom of the case. There was a water stain about palm-sized, edges already dry, center still wet. He touched it with his finger, sticky, carrying an indescribable sweet fishiness.

"Leaking?" Xiong Dali asked.

Xiong Houshi shook his head: "Doesn't look like it. Looks like it flowed from somewhere else." He looked up at the cargo pile above, flashlight shining upward. The cases above were all dry, no leak traces. He thought about it, put his nose close to the water stain and sniffed. The sweet fishiness was stronger, mixed with chemical sting.

"Record it," he said.

Xiong Dali pulled out his record board from his back pocket—his was specially made, one size larger than normal, otherwise his thick fingers couldn't hold the pencil. He wrote crookedly with a short pencil: Area B-7, bottom wooden crate has wet stain, sweet fishy smell, suspected liquid leakage.

Finished writing, the two lifted the case back to its original position. Putting it back was harder than taking it down, because they had to align it precisely with the original position, not crooked, not tilted. Xiong Houshi's arm muscles bulged block by block, deltoids, biceps, triceps, latissimus dorsi, all participating in exerting force, the green uniform stretched almost to bursting. Sweat seeped from his forehead, flowing down the white fur into his eyes, stinging painfully. But he didn't stop, biting his teeth, adjusting the case's angle bit by bit until it fit back perfectly.

"Hoo—" Putting down the case, he exhaled deeply, wiping his face with his sleeve. The sleeve was already soaked through, wiping only made it more sticky.

The two continued their patrol. The alleyways in Area B were wider than Area A's, because the cases were large, needing space for moving. But still dim, still silent. The flashlight beam cut a trembling path of light in the darkness, beyond which was blackness too thick to dissolve. Xiong Houshi's back was completely soaked through, the green uniform sticking tightly to his skin, outlining thick back muscles and round waistline. He could feel sweat flowing down his spinal groove, into his waistband, soaking the edges of his white socks.

His feet also began to hurt. Not from tiredness, but from long standing and walking, soles being反震 by the hard concrete floor. His soles were broad, with thick pads on the bottom, supposed to be very durable. But walking continuously for two hours, it still hurt. He stamped his feet, the sound exploding in the emptiness, traveling far, then bouncing back, like another him stamping feet in the distance.

Xiong Dali was behind him, breathing growing increasingly rough. Brown bear beastkin had less endurance than polar bears, tired more easily. Xiong Houshi slowed his pace, waiting for him to catch up.

"Rest a bit?" Xiong Dali asked, voice carrying fatigue.

Xiong Houshi nodded. The two stopped beside a thick cement pillar, leaning against it. The pillar was ice-cold, transmitting through the sweat-soaked uniform to his back, making Xiong Houshi shudder. He fumbled out his water flask from inside his shirt—specially made extra-large, holding two liters. Unscrewed the cap, tilted his head and gulped several mouthfuls. The water was lukewarm now, carrying the taste of the plastic container. But he drank very satisfied, Adam's apple rolling up and down, making gulping sounds.

Xiong Dali also drank, more urgently, half spilling on his chest, soaking a patch of his green uniform.

The two leaned together, like two fuzzy little mountains. Their body temperatures were both high, leaning together the heat transferred to each other, the surrounding air warming up. Xiong Houshi could smell Xiong Dali's scent—the brown bear's unique, richer body odor, mixed with sweat and faint earthy fishiness. This smell reminded him of the mountain forests of his hometown, of childhood times rolling in the forest with other little bears.

"Houshi," Xiong Dali suddenly spoke, voice sounding very muffled in the darkness, "Do you miss home?"

Xiong Houshi was stunned. Miss home? He rarely did. Or rather, he didn't dare to. Once he thought about it, his heart felt empty, like a piece was missing.

"Sometimes," he answered honestly.

"I miss it too," Xiong Dali said, voice lowering, "My ma's honey cakes, so delicious. Can't buy that taste outside."

Xiong Houshi thought of his mother's fish. Polar bears ate seals and fish, his mother's specialty was grilled fish—whole big fish, scales not removed, directly grilled over fire, grilled until crispy outside and tender inside, fish meat sweet and fresh, fish oil sizzling. He swallowed saliva, his stomach rumbling appropriately.

"When we get our pay, I'll go buy a fish," he said, "The cafeteria fish isn't good, no flavor."

"I'll go buy honey," Xiong Dali said, "Dip buns in it."

The two were silent for a while, imagining the taste of fish and honey. In the darkness, only their heavy breathing and the faint dripping sound in the distance.

Rested enough, the two continued patrolling. Reaching the depths of Area B, Xiong Houshi suddenly stopped.

"Wait," he said, nose inhaling forcefully in one direction.

Xiong Dali immediately halted, hand moving to the baton at his waist.

Xiong Houshi walked toward the scent source. It was a pile of metal cases in the furthest corner of Area B, stacked more neatly than elsewhere, the seals also newer. He leaned close to one case, nose almost pressed to the gap between the case panels.

"This smell..." he frowned.

"What smell?" Xiong Dali asked, also leaning in to smell.

"Can't say for sure," Xiong Houshi sniffed again, "A bit sweet, a bit fishy, and... a chemical sting. All mixed together." He stepped back, shining the flashlight on the batch number and seals of these metal cases. The numbers were consecutive, the seals intact, but the smell was wrong.

Xiong Dali also crouched to inspect. His eyes reflected faintly in the dim light, brown bears also had decent night vision. "Seals haven't been touched," he observed for a while, then pushed the case with his hand. The case didn't budge, very heavy. "What's inside? So heavy."

"Don't know," Xiong Houshi said, "Record it."

He added another note to his record board: Area B-12, metal case pile, seals intact but unusual mixed odor detected, cases heavy.

Finished writing, he looked at those cases, feeling a bit uneasy. This smell reminded him of some bad memories—when working at a northern port before, he'd seen smuggled chemical leaks, it was this sweet-fishy with sting smell. Several coworkers had been poisoned then, foaming at the mouth, carried away and never came back.

But he didn't say it. Saying it would be useless, would only make Xiong Dali worry. He patted Xiong Dali's shoulder: "Let's go, continue."

The two continued forward. But Xiong Houshi couldn't shake that strange smell from his mind. Sweet, fishy, chemical. He shook his head in the darkness, throwing off these thoughts. Can't think wildly. In Beastshield, the less you know, the longer you live.

The flashlight beam continued cutting through the darkness. The shadows of cargo cases stretched and twisted in the light, like monsters about to pounce at any moment. Xiong Houshi's ears stood upright, capturing every sound beyond their footsteps. In the distance was a dripping sound, very regular, probably a leaking pipe somewhere. Further away, there seemed to be an extremely faint, metallic creaking sound, but it was indistinct.

His heartbeat sped up a bit. Not from fear, from tension. Every time he smelled strange odors, heard strange sounds, he would tense. This was instinct—in the wild, strange smells and sounds often meant danger.

But he forced himself to relax. This wasn't the wild, this was a warehouse. Though this warehouse was a bit strange, it was still a man-made place, with rules, with companions, with a foreman. Even if there really was danger, it wouldn't be something they two ordinary guards could handle. What they could do was record, report, then wait.

Reaching the end of Area B, light appeared ahead—it was the passage to Area C, where there was a safety light. Dim yellow light seeped over from there, casting a fuzzy halo on the ground.

Xiong Houshi turned off his flashlight. Eyes adapted for a moment to the slightly brighter light. He glanced at his record board, three abnormalities already recorded: stain on case bottom, metal case unusual odor, the strange sound from just now.

"Almost done," he said, "Let's head back."

Xiong Dali nodded, obviously relieved. The two turned around, walking back along the way they came. The return journey felt shorter than the way there, perhaps because of familiarity, perhaps because knowing the exit was ahead.

Reaching the warehouse main gate, morning sunlight leaked in through the door gap, forming a dazzling band of light. Xiong Houshi pushed the door open, light instantly flooding in, like a tide drowning them.

Outside, the sky was already fully bright.

## Scene-4 Noon Fullness and Drowsiness

Coming out of the warehouse, the sun was at its fiercest.

The front courtyard concrete ground was blinding white, reflecting harsh light, like a shattered mirror. Xiong Houshi squinted his eyes—polar bear eyes feared strong light. He and Xiong Dali walked to the faucet, where several people were already lined up.

The water was pumped from underground, ice-cold. Xiong Houshi directly stuck his furry head under the faucet. Water rushing on his scalp made him shudder, but then came relief—cooled down. He washed his face, neck, then pulled open his uniform front, letting water rush into his chest. Water flowed down his dense chest fur, washing away sweat salt, leaving water traces on the white fur, immediately evaporated by the heat, the fur fluffing up again.

He could feel the sunlight baking his back through the uniform, the skin beneath the fabric beginning to burn. White fur reflected sunlight, making him more heat-tolerant than dark-furred beastkin, but still hot. He unzipped his collar, letting heat escape a bit, revealing a large patch of furry chest. The chest muscle contours were faintly visible beneath the fur, not that kind of segmented muscle, but one thick slab, rising and falling with breath.

Xiong Dali was panting beside him, brown bears even less heat-tolerant, tongue hanging out, huffing like a dog. His fur was dark and light patches from sweat, sticking tightly to his skin, making muscle blocks even more defined.

"So hot..." Xiong Dali muttered, directly pouring a whole bucket of water over his head.

Xiong Houshi said nothing, just continued rinsing. Finished, he walked to the shade at the warehouse's back wall, where there was a small patch of shadow. He sat down with a thud, the concrete floor making a dull sound. He sat against the wall, the concrete transmitting slight coolness through the uniform. He fumbled out his water flask from inside his shirt, drinking a few more mouthfuls. The water was already warm, carrying the taste of the plastic container.

Lunchtime. The cafeteria food cart was pushed over, wheels creaking. Pushing it was a chubby raccoon beastkin uncle, apron so oily it shone, could reflect light.

Lunch was slightly better than breakfast. Besides thin porridge and buns, there was a spoonful of watery stewed cabbage, floating two fingernail-sized slices of fatty meat. Each person also had a boiled egg—Wednesday's fixed benefit.

Xiong Houshi collected his portion, walking back to sit in the shade. He didn't eat immediately, but first looked. This was his habit: first appreciate the food, then eat. He stared at the contents of his lunch box: the thin porridge was cloudy, but you could see a few rice grains; the buns were gray, but there were two, larger than breakfast; the cabbage was bland, but it was vegetables after all; most enticing were those two fatty meat slices, though only fingernail-sized, glistening with oil in the sunlight, like two small pieces of amber.

And the egg. He carefully picked up the egg, holding it in his palm. The egg was warm, carrying the residual heat of the cafeteria's large pot. He didn't plan to eat the egg immediately today, saving it for when he was hungriest. That was his energy reserve, his secret weapon.

He began eating. First a sip of porridge, moistening his throat. The porridge had little flavor, just cloudy water with a hint of scorched pot-bottom taste. Then he ate the buns. He broke open a bun, sandwiching one fatty meat slice inside. The fatty meat's grease seeped into the dry hard bun, making it oily and moist, texture much improved. He bit in big mouthfuls, chewing very hard, cheeks bulging like two balls.

Gou Laifu came over with his lunch box, sitting down beside him. "Brother Houshi, you eat so deliciously," the puppy beastkin said, eyes glancing at the fatty meat slice in Xiong Houshi's lunch box.

Xiong Houshi looked at him, hesitated, then broke off a small piece from his bun, with a bit of fatty meat grease on it, handing it over: "Here."

Gou Laifu was stunned, ears perking up: "Brother Houshi, this..."

"You eat," Xiong Houshi smiled honestly, "I have enough."

Gou Laifu accepted that small piece of bun, eating it in small bites, each bite chewed carefully, as if tasting some delicacy. Xiong Houshi watched him, feeling a bit happy. Sharing food was joyful, especially sharing when you weren't well-off yourself, that joy doubled.

He continued eating his own. The cabbage had little flavor, salt was stingy, stewed mushy, chopsticks夹起来就碎. But he ate very seriously, not missing a single leaf. With the cabbage, he finished both buns. Then drank the porridge, licking the last drops from the bottom of the lunch box.

Finally, time to eat the egg. But he wasn't in a hurry. He held the egg in his palm, feeling its temperature. He wanted to save it, for when he was tired and hungry in the afternoon. That was his energy reserve, his secret weapon.

Huang Biao came over, grass stem dangling from his mouth, the scar on his face looking particularly ferocious in the noon sunlight. "Yo, silly big guy, eating quite a lot today," he grinned, smile carrying malice.

Xiong Houshi ignored him, lowering his head to pack up his lunch box.

Huang Biao stared at the egg in his hand: "Not eating the egg? Not eating, give it to me."

Xiong Houshi gripped the egg tighter, protecting it against his chest: "I'm eating. Will eat later."

"Stingy," Huang Biao sneered, "A lousy egg, treating it like treasure."

Xiong Houshi still didn't make a sound. He knew arguing with Huang Biao was useless, would only invite more mockery. He stood up, carrying his lunch box to wash at the faucet. Huang Biao watched him walk away, found it boring, and left too.

While washing his lunch box, Xiong Houshi ran into Niu Dazhuang. Niu Dazhuang was carefully scrubbing his own lunch box, that dented lid he scrubbed very seriously, edges all scrubbed.

"Brother Niu," Xiong Houshi greeted, "Hot today."

"Yeah," Niu Dazhuang nodded, looking up at him, "You full?"

"Full," Xiong Houshi patted his belly, making dull thumping sounds, "Just... still want some meat."

Niu Dazhuang smiled, smile honest: "When we get our pay, go buy some."

"Yeah!" Xiong Houshi nodded vigorously.

Finished washing his lunch box, he placed it upside down on the windowsill to dry. Then walked back to the shade, sitting against the wall. Two hours for noon break, he could take a nap. He closed his eyes, but didn't sleep immediately. First he listened to the sounds around: distant cicada chirps, continuous, making people anxious; nearby other beastkin eating, chatting sounds; further away, faint life noises from town.

He could feel sunlight through his eyelids, a warm red. Heat accumulated in his body, very comfortable, like sunbathing. Polar bears liked sunbathing, it was how they supplemented vitamin D. He adjusted his posture, letting sunlight hit his back more. Warm sunlight through the uniform, hitting his skin, warm and cozy, like mother's hand caressing.

He gradually relaxed. Muscles melted like butter spreading out, bones relaxed, breathing became deep and even. He entered a half-asleep half-awake state, consciousness floating on water, drifting with the current.

He dreamed of his hometown. Dreamed of ice fields, dreamed of the sea, dreamed of mother's grilled fish, oil sizzling, fragrance filling the air. He dreamed of playing in the snow with younger siblings, fur covered in snow, like a moving snowball. He dreamed of hunting seals, quietly waiting on the ice, then pouncing...

"Houshi! Houshi!"

Someone was pushing him. Xiong Houshi jerked awake, opened his eyes. It was Xiong Dali, shaking his shoulder hard.

"Wha... what's wrong?" Xiong Houshi asked indistinctly, mouth still full of saliva.

"Time to work," Xiong Dali said, "The whistle blew, you didn't hear it?"

Xiong Houshi sat up, rubbing his eyes. Indeed, the whistle's lingering sound still echoed in the air. He'd slept so deeply, hadn't even heard the whistle. He shook his head, making himself awake. Then stood up, patting the dust from his clothes.

The afternoon work was unloading and loading cargo.

## Scene-5 The Dance of Muscles

A blue truck waited in the center of the courtyard, engine not turned off, idling with a low rumble, exhaust pipe spewing faint black smoke, mixing in the scorching air, forming distorted heat waves. The driver was a hyena beastkin, leaning against the door smoking, seeing them come out, lifted his chin, considered a greeting. He was tall and thin, fur dirty, green uniform worn loosely, eyes alert and tired.

The delivery slip was on the foreman office windowsill. Bao Su went to get it. Xiong Houshi and Xiong Dali stood beside the truck waiting. Xiong Houshi moved his shoulders and arms, preparing for the upcoming heavy physical labor. He could feel his muscles warming up, blood flowing to his limbs, heartbeat quickening, breathing deepening. This was his body entering work mode signals.

Bao Su returned with the slip. Area D, numbers D-22 to D-25, twenty cases of "hardware parts." Also a batch of newly arrived "mechanical equipment" to be stored.

The six divided into two groups. Niu Dazhuang, Xiong Houshi, Xiong Dali were responsible for moving out the cases to be picked up. Gou Laifu, Bao Su, Huang Biao were responsible for inventory checking and supervising loading, then receiving new cargo.

Xiong Houshi walked into the warehouse, heading to Area D. Area D was at the deepest part of the warehouse, darker and colder than other areas, the smell also more complex. Here were all the heavy stuff: metal parts, mechanical components, bundles of steel bars. Cases were larger than Area B's, heavier.

Niu Dazhuang had already started moving. Xiong Houshi watched him bend down, thick arms passing through the gaps at the bottom of the case, lifting it up. When the case left the ground, Niu Dazhuang's leg muscles tensed, pants stretched tight, outlining thigh muscle contours. Back muscles bulged, straining the soaked green uniform into clear lines, sweat forming dark water stains beneath the fabric. Weight pressed on his arms, bones making slight cracking sounds.

Xiong Houshi also found a case. He crouched down, arms passing through the bottom of the case. The case was very heavy, at least one hundred fifty jin. He took a deep breath, legs exerting force, waist and abdomen tightening, arms straining. The case left the ground. He could feel weight pressing on his arms and shoulders, muscle fibers tensing to the extreme in an instant, then adapting, then stabilizing.

He stepped forward, walking toward the truck stopped in the sunlight outside the door. Footsteps heavy, each step leaving dull thuds on the concrete floor. His heartbeat accelerated, breathing grew rough, sweat seeping from his forehead, flowing down the white fur into his eyes, stinging painfully. But he didn't stop, continued walking. One step, two steps, three steps... reaching the truck, he lifted the case, steadily placing it in the truck bed. Putting it down, he exhaled deeply, white mist instantly dissipating in the scorching air.

Then turned around, went back to move the next one.

This was his work, also his strength. He had power, had endurance, had thick muscles and bones to bear weight. When moving heavy objects, he could feel every part of his body working in coordination: legs providing thrust, waist transmitting force, shoulders and arms bearing weight, hands providing grip. This was a precise system, and he was the operator of this system.

He liked this feeling—body fully used, muscles fully stretched and contracted, sweat fully expelled. This made him feel alive, useful, valuable.

Second case, third case... he moved faster and faster, more and more skilled. Sweat flowed like small streams from his body, thoroughly soaking the green uniform, sticking tightly to his skin, outlining every muscle's rise and fall: thick chest muscles, round abdominal muscles, thick arm muscles, broad back muscles, solid thigh muscles. He was like a moving muscle sculpture, shuttling back and forth between warehouse and truck.

Xiong Dali was also moving, one case at a time, footsteps solid, but breathing growing increasingly rough, like a broken blower. Xiong Houshi passed by him, heard him mutter: "Tired... so tired..."

"Rest a bit?" Xiong Houshi asked.

Xiong Dali shook his head: "No need... I can do it."

Xiong Houshi patted his shoulder, continuing to move his own. He knew Xiong Dali was proud, unwilling to show weakness.

Reaching the tenth case, Xiong Houshi felt his arms beginning to ache. Lactic acid accumulated in his muscles, bringing familiar burning sensation. But he didn't stop. He clenched his teeth, continuing to move. Eleventh case, twelfth case... his breathing grew increasingly rough, heartbeat like drumming, sweat flowing into his eyes, stinging, vision somewhat blurred. But he still didn't stop. He counted: thirteen, fourteen, fifteen...

This was a self-challenge. He wanted to see where his limit was, wanted to see how many cases he could move, how long he could persist. This was silly, he knew. No one cared how many cases he moved, the foreman wouldn't give him an extra cent, companions wouldn't praise him extra. But he just wanted to know, wanted to prove—prove he had strength, had value, wasn't a silly big guy eating for free.

Eighteenth case. His arms began to tremble. Not from fear, but from muscle fatigue reaching the extreme physiological reaction. He clenched his teeth, forehead veins bulging, eyes bloodshot, skin beneath white fur flushed red. He picked up the case, stepped forward. Footsteps staggered, nearly falling. But he stabilized, continued walking. One step, two steps, three steps... reaching the truck, he lifted the case. Arms trembling badly, the case swaying in the air. He took a deep breath, using his last bit of strength, pushing the case into the truck bed.

Putting down the case, he was nearly exhausted. Hands on knees, breathing heavily, sweat pouring like a waterfall from his face, dripping on the ground, instantly evaporated by the scorching concrete floor. He could feel his heart beating crazily in his chest, like it was about to explode. Lungs burned with pain, each breath like inhaling flames.

But he did it. Twenty cases, he moved twelve, Niu Dazhuang moved six, Xiong Dali moved two. He did it.

He straightened up, wiping his face. Hands full of sweat, wiping was useless. He walked back to the shade, sitting down with his back against the wall. The concrete floor was burning hot, but he didn't care. He needed rest, needed cooling, needed to replenish water.

He fumbled out his water flask from inside his shirt, unscrewed the cap, tilting his head and drinking heavily. The water was already warm, but he drank urgently, half spilling on his chest, soaking the already soaked green uniform again. After drinking, he exhaled deeply, feeling much better.

Then he remembered the egg. That one he'd been saving, as energy reserve. He fumbled out the egg from his pocket, already warmed by his body temperature. He carefully peeled it—much clumsier than in the morning, fingers trembling from fatigue. Peeled, he stuffed the whole egg into his mouth, chewing.

Egg white smooth and tender, yolk dense and creamy, with faint fishy and salty taste. Eating this egg after extreme fatigue, the feeling was indescribable—like dry land welcoming rain, like a hungry stomach receiving comfort. He closed his eyes, chewing carefully, letting egg fragrance fill his entire mouth, slide down his esophagus, warm his whole stomach, then diffuse throughout his body, replenishing the depleted energy.

He hummed contentedly, tail patting the ground lightly behind him—though very short, almost invisible, he could feel it.

At this time, Gou Laifu and Bao Su began inventorying the newly arrived cargo. The new cargo was also a pile of cases, seals brand new, several case corners had collision marks. Gou Laifu was recording seriously, Bao Su was inspecting seals.

Xiong Houshi watched them, feeling a bit envious. He envied Gou Laifu's dexterity, envied Bao Su's meticulousness. He himself only knew how to exert strength, clumsy, besides moving things, knew nothing.

But he quickly became relieved again. Strength was also a skill, right? He could move things others couldn't move, could bear weights others couldn't bear. Wasn't that value?

He stood up, patting the dust from his buttocks. Though tired, his mood was good. He'd completed his work, challenged himself, and eaten a delicious egg. Today was good.

The sun began to set in the west. Sunset clouds dyed the sky orange-red, the warehouse's tin roof reflecting warm light, but Xiong Houshi knew that warmth was false. Once the sun set, it would get cold again.

In the front courtyard, the day shift people gathered, waiting for shift change. Xiong Houshi handed his record board to the night shift foreman—still that wild boar beastkin, yawning.

"Recorded the Area B abnormalities?" the boar foreman glanced at the record board.

"Recorded," Xiong Houshi said.

"Mm." The foreman didn't ask further, waving his hand, "Alright, scram."

Shift change complete. Xiong Houshi returned to the group, seeing Niu Dazhuang just tucking that lunch box containing money back under the bed. Gou Laifu sidled up to Bao Su, saying something softly, tail wagging in small arcs. Huang Biao leaned against the wall, eyes closed, whether sleeping or awake unclear. Xiong Dali was already瘫坐在地上, thick legs spread out, like two tree trunks.

The six dispersed in their own directions. Xiong Houshi was the last to leave the front courtyard. He looked back at the warehouse. The main gate was being slowly closed by the night shift people, the iron doors meeting with a heavy, prolonged sound, like a sigh.

Then, the lock clicked shut.

## Scene-6 Night Warmth and Dreams

Dinner was still the same. Thin porridge, hard steamed buns, pickled vegetables.

Xiong Houshi ate quickly. He was hungry, the afternoon physical consumption too great, the noon food long since digested. The porridge was too thin, he drank it in a few gulps, buns with pickled vegetables, chewing until his jaw ached. He ate three buns—the cafeteria uncle gave him an extra one, because he worked hard in the afternoon.

After dinner, he helped the cafeteria uncle collect lunch boxes, earning a mumbled "thanks." Then he returned to the dormitory, where the oil lamp was already lit.

The dormitory was filled with the black smoke of burning oil lamps and the thick body odor of gathered male beastkin. Xiong Houshi walked to his bed, not lying down immediately. He first took off his boots. The boots were heavy, soles covered in dust and sweat stains. He placed the boots neatly under the bed, then took off his socks. The white long socks were already soaked through, the bottom printed with his number: G-535, but blurred from sweat. He rolled up the socks, placing them on the windowsill to dry—would wear them tomorrow.

Then he took off his clothes. The green uniform had been repeatedly soaked through and air-dried, stiff and hard, covered with a layer of salt碱, feeling沙沙 when touched. He carefully took it off, folded it neatly, placing it beside the bed. Then he sat on the bed edge, bare-chested, wearing only a pair of loose shorts.

His body under the oil lamp looked large and furry. White fur covered his entire body, glowing with soft luster in the light. Chest muscles thick, rising and falling with breath, fur rippling accordingly. The abdominal fat layer bulged roundly, but pressing with hand it was hard, not soft fat. Arms thick, muscle lines faintly visible beneath the fur. Back muscles broad, like a furry shield.

He dragged out his "treasure chest" from under the bed—an old ammunition case, don't know where he picked it up from, paint peeling, revealing rust-red base. He opened the case, inside was his entire belongings: several changes of old clothes, all washed white; a worn belt, patched in several places; a small jar of fish oil, sent from hometown, he couldn't bear to eat it, only licking a small bit each day; and a photo, a family portrait, parents and three younger siblings, photo already yellowed, edges curling.

He picked up the photo, looking at it by oil lamp light. In the photo, parents smiled very kindly, younger siblings still small, nestled beside parents. He himself stood at the very edge, back then he was still young, fur whiter and brighter, light in his eyes. He looked at the photo for a long time, then carefully put it back.

Then he took out that jar of fish oil from the case. Unscrewed the lid, the fish oil's fishy fragrance immediately floated out, rich, carrying ocean's breath. He dipped a little with his pinky finger, placing it on the tip of his tongue. Fish oil was slippery, salty-fishy, but aftertaste was sweet. This was hometown's taste, was mother's taste. He closed his eyes, slowly savoring, letting that taste diffuse in his mouth, then slide down his throat, warming his stomach.

Licking a bit of fish oil before bed each day was his ritual. This made him feel at ease, feel that he was still that little bear from the north, not completely assimilated by this strange place.

After licking, he tightened the lid, putting it back in the case. Then he lay on the bed. The bed board was hard, pressing against the thick fat and muscles on his back, but he was used to it. He even felt this hardness helped maintain the physiological curvature of his spine. He pulled over the thin blanket, covering his belly—belly feared cold, other places didn't.

The oil lamp was still burning, flame flickering, casting swaying shadows on the wall. The dormitory was noisy—Niu Dazhuang was writing a letter, pencil scratching on paper; Gou Laifu was looking at his "Shop Opening Guide" in the corner, page-turning sound very light; Huang Biao was lying on the bed, hands behind his head, staring at the swaying lamp shadows on the ceiling; Bao Su was doing evening stretches in the corner, movements smooth and silent; Xiong Dali was already asleep, snores even, like a well-running engine.

Xiong Houshi looked at all this, feeling a simple satisfaction in his heart. Everyone was here, everyone was safe, everyone was alive. That was enough.

He thought of today's work. Thought of the muscle aches when moving those heavy cases, thought of the sting when sweat flowed into his eyes, thought of the exhaustion after putting down the cases, thought of the deliciousness of that egg eaten after extreme fatigue. These were all real, concrete, touchable. This made him feel alive, useful.

He also thought of those abnormalities. The wet stain on the case bottom, the strange smell from the metal cases, the strange sound in the darkness. These made him uneasy, but also made him feel his importance—if he didn't inspect, didn't record, what if something really happened? Though he knew reporting might not get anyone's attention, at least he did what he should.

He thought of Gou Laifu's dream. Opening a shop, selling cigarettes, alcohol, snacks. A very humble dream, but very real. He thought of Gou Laifu eating the bun he gave with that cherishing look, feeling a bit sour, and a bit warm. He thought, when he saved enough money, maybe he could lend some to Gou Laifu, help him realize his dream.

He thought of Niu Dazhuang. Honest, silent, sending money home on time every month. Niu Dazhuang's shoulders were very broad, very thick, could bear very heavy cases, could also bear very heavy life. Xiong Houshi respected Niu Dazhuang, felt he was a true man.

He thought of Bao Su. Elegant, calm, doing everything methodically. Xiong Houshi was a bit afraid of Bao Su, but more was respect. He felt Bao Su wasn't an ordinary person, definitely had a story. But he didn't ask, Bao Su didn't say. This was fine.

He thought of Huang Biao. Cynical, full of thorns. Xiong Houshi didn't understand Huang Biao, but didn't hate him either. He felt Huang Biao had wounds in his heart, that's why he was like that. Beastkin with wounds needed to be tolerated.

He thought of Xiong Dali. Honest, simple, his good partner. Being with Xiong Dali, he felt relaxed, didn't need to think too much, didn't need to pretend, could be his truest self.

This was his world. Not large, but complete. Not exciting, but real. Had hardship, had fatigue, had unease, but also had warmth, had satisfaction, had small happiness.

He yawned, tears squeezing out. Sleepy. Today was too tired, body needed rest.

He blew out the oil lamp. The dormitory sank into darkness, only a bit of hazy starlight seeping in from the window, and a bit of glow from the distant warehouse safety light. Darkness was like a warm thick blanket, wrapping around him, making him feel safe.

He closed his eyes, adjusting his breathing. Breathing gradually became even, deep. Body completely relaxed, muscles melting like wax spreading out on the bed. Consciousness began to blur, like ink drops in water, slowly spreading, fading, dissipating.

He dreamed of his hometown. Dreamed of ice fields, endless white. Dreamed of the sea, deep blue, waves rising and falling. Dreamed of mother, standing at the doorway waving at him, holding freshly grilled fish in her hand, oil sizzling, fragrance filling the air. Dreamed of younger siblings, rolling in the snow, laughter crisp and clear.

He smiled, in his sleep. Corner of mouth slightly curving up, revealing an honest smile.

Tomorrow, would still be the same day. Get up early, breakfast, patrol, lunch, moving cargo, dinner, sleep.

But it didn't matter. Such days, he could live for a long, long time.

As long as everyone was still here.

As long as there was still food to eat.

As long as he could still lick a bit of hometown fish oil before bed.

As long as he could still dream of mother and grilled fish.

That was enough.

(The End of Xiong Houshi's Day)
