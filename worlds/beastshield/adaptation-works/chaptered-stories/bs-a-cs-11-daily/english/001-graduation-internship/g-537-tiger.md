# Huang Biao's Day

## Scene-1 The Scar's Awakening

Pain woke before consciousness.

It was the scar on his face hurting—not sharp stabbing pain, but a deep, dull, throbbing ache buried beneath the flesh, pulsing with his heartbeat. Huang Biao opened his eyes in the darkness, the first thing being using his tongue to push against the inside of his right cheek. There was a corresponding, uneven hard lump there, a trace left by the scar inside his mouth. Eight years, this pain was like an old friend, visiting punctually every day.

The dormitory was still dark, only a few strands of iron-gray light leaking through the gaps in the tattered burlap nailed over the windows. The air was thick as porridge, the exhaled waste gas of twelve male beastkin, the saltiness after sweat evaporation, the faint sourness of fur grease fermenting overnight, and that rotting-meat-like mustiness wafting from the pile of dirty socks in the corner, all mixed together, pressing heavily on the chest.

He lay still, first counting the shadows of the roof beams. One, two, three... counting to the seventh, from the lower bunk came Gou Laifu's fine teeth-grinding sounds, crunch crunch, like a rat gnawing at his brain. On the upper right, Bao Su as usual made no sound, but Huang Biao knew he was there—the black panther beastkin had a special kind of stillness when sleeping, like a predator lying in wait before striking, making people's backs hair-raising.

He slowly sat up, the iron bed frame emitting a sharp groan. This sound he knew, just like he knew how every old injury on his body ached on rainy days—the凹陷 left by a knife chop on the left shoulder, the凸起 from the healed fracture of the third rib on the right side, and that circular scar with皱缩 edges on his calf from a gunshot wound. Inside the boot was a hidden pocket, containing his most valuable possession: a switchblade, blade only palm-length, but fast enough, concealed enough.

He put on the boots, not tying the laces—tying laces was something kids and softies did. Then put on that green uniform. Enamel, with a faded flower printed on the bottom, edges chipped in several places. Toothbrush bristles nearly bald, toothpaste squeezing out grayish-white, carrying a chemical fragrance.

## Scene-2 Breakfast and Cigarettes

Breakfast time.

Six lunch boxes arranged on the square table, contents unchanged for ten years: soup thin enough to count rice grains, two bran-mixed gray steamed buns, a pinch of black pickled vegetable strips. And one boiled egg per person—Wednesday's charity.

Huang Biao collected his portion, didn't find a place to sit, directly stood against the wall eating. He stuffed the buns into his mouth in three or two mouthfuls, chewing with cheeks bulging, that scar twisting up and down with the chewing motion. Then he peeled the egg, threw the whole thing into his mouth, chewed twice and swallowed, Adam's apple rolling roughly. The soup was warm, he tilted his head and poured it down, half spilling on his chest, soaking a patch of his green uniform.

Finished eating, he casually threw the lunch box on the table, a clang. Then fumbled out a cigarette pack from his pants pocket—flat, shake it, only half a cigarette rolling inside. He poured it out, the cigarette body wrinkled, filter end still stained with some unknown dirt. He lit it, inhaling deeply, smoke spurting from nostrils and corners of mouth simultaneously.

"Brother Biao, smoke less, it's bad for your health," Gou Laifu said softly, nibbling his egg in small bites.

Huang Biao slanted his eyes at him, smoke swirling before his eyes: "Puppy, is it your business?"

Gou Laifu shrank his neck, not speaking anymore.

Huang Biao continued smoking, gaze sweeping over the people in the room. Niu Dazhuang was eating buns with his own chili paste, eating focused, forehead sweating. Bao Su was eating quietly, movements regular like completing a ritual. Xiong Houshi and Xiong Dali were competing to see who ate faster, two bear heads close together,呼噜呼噜响.

All trash. Huang Biao thought. Niu Dazhuang honest and simple, whole life just thinking about sending that little money home. Gou Laifu daydreaming about opening a shop. Bao Su pretending to be deep, who knows what he's really like behind the scenes. Two bear beastkin, one silly one stupid.

And himself? Thirty-one years old, face carrying a scar, body carrying more scars, mixing at a Beastshield warehouse for three years, receiving a hundred lubi per month, living in a doghouse with twelve people per room, eating pig-food-like meals. Future? No future. Tomorrow? No tomorrow.

Smoked to the end, filter burning his hand. He threw the cigarette butt on the ground, grinding it out with his boot sole. Then walked to Niu Dazhuang's side, crouching down.

"Brother Niu, sending money today?"

Niu Dazhuang nodded, not raising his eyes: "Yeah."

"How much?"

"Eighty."

Huang Biao sneered: "Saved for four months, right? Brother Niu, what are you doing it for? Your family that one, maybe already ran off with someone else."

Niu Dazhuang's movements paused, then continued eating bun, voice muffled: "Don't talk nonsense."

"I'm talking nonsense?" Huang Biao stood up, hands in pockets, "In this world, who the fuck still talks about loyalty? Having money makes you dad, having milk makes you mom. That little money of yours, what can it do sent back? Buy medicine? Buy rice? Not enough for shit."

He spoke loudly, the room quieted down. Gou Laifu uneasily moved his tail. Bao Su raised his head, looked at Huang Biao, gaze calm, but Huang Biao felt there was something beneath that calm, making him uncomfortable.

"Biao, say less," Niu Dazhuang finally raised his eyes to look at him, no anger in his gaze, only a deep exhaustion, "Everyone has their own way of living."

Huang Biao opened his mouth, wanted to say something, but ultimately just snorted, turning and walking away. The skin around the scar began to throb with pain again.

The whistle blew.

## Scene-3 Echoes in the Darkness

Warehouse Area C, Huang Biao and Niu Dazhuang as a team.

Pushing open the door, darkness surged out, carrying familiar smells—dust, rust, machine oil, and some deeper layer, like countless secrets rotting together. Huang Biao flicked on his flashlight, beam stabbing into darkness, only illuminating a small patch before him. He liked this feeling—darkness was like a protective color, could hide many things, including the scar on his face, including expressions he didn't want people to see.

The two walked one after another inside. Footsteps amplified in the emptiness, hitting distant cargo piles, bouncing back, forming continuous echoes. Huang Biao walked not fast, flashlight beam sweeping left and right, more habitual action than really inspecting. His mind was still on Niu Dazhuang's words—everyone has their own way of living.

Fuck that way of living. Huang Biao thought. His way of living was muddling through. Muddling through on the streets, muddling through in Beastshield, muddling through one day at a time. When he couldn't muddle through anymore, at worst it was one lousy life, throw it in some stinking ditch, whoever wants to collect it can collect it.

Flashlight beam swept across a batch of wooden crates. Huang Biao casually poked a crate corner with his baton, listening to the echo. Thud, dull sound. Normal. He continued forward.

"Biao," Niu Dazhuang spoke from behind, voice sounding a bit飘 in the darkness, "Your face injury... how did it happen?"

Huang Biao's footsteps paused. The skin around the scar suddenly tightened, pain becoming clear. He licked that hard lump inside his mouth.

"None of your business," he said.

"Just asking," Niu Dazhuang said, voice still like that, unhurried, "Seeing you're not that old, how..."

"How did I end up in this shitty state?" Huang Biao took over, turning around, flashlight beam shining straight on Niu Dazhuang's face. Niu Dazhuang squinted, but didn't dodge. "Let me tell you, Brother Niu, in this world, it's not that if you work honestly you'll have a good ending. This scar on my face, it's from believing someone I shouldn't have believed, helping with something I shouldn't have helped with, in exchange."

His voice wasn't loud, but in the silent warehouse, every word was crystal clear.

"Eight years ago, I was mixing in the southern part of the city. There was an old big brother, treated me not bad, gave me food to eat, gave me a place to sleep. Later he fought with someone over territory, called me to help. I went, brought weapons." Huang Biao made a chopping motion with his hand, "The other side had more people, more ruthless. The old big brother ran, threw me there. This knife," he pointed at the scar on his face, "came from the left. I dodged a bit, otherwise my head would have been split open."

He paused, taking a breath. In the darkness, only dust dancing in the flashlight beam.

"And then?" Niu Dazhuang asked.

"And then?" Huang Biao laughed, laughter dry, "Then I lay in a garbage heap for a whole night, blood flowing half my body. In the morning discovered by a scavenging old man, dragged to a black clinic, seventeen stitches. No money to pay medical fees, the clinic bastard扒了我的 last decent coat to settle the debt. Later, wound healed, scar stayed, old big brother never appeared again."

He spoke calmly, like telling someone else's story. But that familiar, icy rage in his chest burned again. Eight years, this rage had never truly extinguished.

Niu Dazhuang was silent for a long time. Then he said: "So you think, everyone is untrustworthy?"

"Otherwise?" Huang Biao turned around, continuing forward, flashlight beam swaying in the darkness, "Brother Niu, you believe your family is waiting for you, believe the money you send back can change anything. I tell you, sooner or later you'll find out you believed wrong. By then, you won't even be as good as me—at least I stopped believing long ago, won't be disappointed."

The two didn't speak anymore. Only footsteps and breathing sounds. Huang Biao's flashlight beam swept across a batch of metal cases, he suddenly stopped.

"Hear that?" he lowered his voice.

Niu Dazhuang listened with tilted ear. In the darkness, there was an extremely faint dripping sound, coming from deep within the metal case pile. Regular, continuous.

"Dripping water?" Niu Dazhuang said.

Huang Biao shook his head. He walked closer a few steps, flashlight beam shining on the case surface. Military green paint, brand new seals. But that dripping sound... didn't sound like water. More viscous, heavier.

He remembered sounds he'd heard when helping at a black market warehouse before—some chemical slowly leaking, dripping on metal container bottom, was this kind of sound.

"Record it," he said, voice a bit tight.

Niu Dazhuang pulled out the record board, writing with pencil. Huang Biao watched from the side, that scar twitching slightly under the dim light.

Finished recording, the two continued patrolling. But Huang Biao's mind was no longer on inspection. Dripping sound, strange sweet fishiness, brand new seals... these details turned in his mind. He wasn't an honest person like Niu Dazhuang, he knew this warehouse absolutely didn't transport just "hardware parts" and "mechanical equipment." What did Beastshield do? Smuggling, contraband, shady deals. This warehouse was remote, guards were lax, practically a perfect transit station.

And he, Huang Biao, number G-537, a former street thug receiving a hundred lubi per month, was right here in this transit station, every day inspecting these cases that could contain anything.

Ridiculous. Pathetic.

Flashlight beam swept across a wall corner, he saw a patch of dark stain on the ground. He crouched down, touching it with his hand. Sticky, dry, but not completely dry. He leaned in to smell—rust smell, sweet fishiness, and... a faint, almost imperceptible chemical sting.

Blood. Blood mixed with something else.

He stood up, patting his hands. Didn't tell Niu Dazhuang. What was the use of recording it? What was the use of reporting it? Would Beastshield care about blood stains discovered by a bottom-level杂兵?

No. They only cared whether this batch of cargo was safely shipped out, whether money was received.

He continued forward, boot soles stepping on concrete floor, making heavy dull sounds. The scar on his face throbbed with the pace, jumping with pain.

## Scene-4 The Noon Dazzle

Coming out of the warehouse, the sun was at its fiercest.

The front courtyard concrete ground was blinding white, reflective light making people unable to open their eyes. Huang Biao walked to the faucet, turned it on, directly stuck his head under the water flow to rinse. Water was ice-cold, rushing on scalp, making him shudder. He washed face, neck, then pulled open his uniform front, letting water rush into his chest. Water flowed down muscle grooves, washing away sweat salt, leaving water traces on dark skin.

He shook his head, water droplets splashing in all directions. Then fumbled out the cigarette pack from his pants pocket, still that half cigarette. He lit it, inhaling deeply, smoke quickly dissipating in the scorching air.

Gou Laifu and Bao Su also came out from Area A. Gou Laifu was panting with tongue hanging out, ears completely drooping. Bao Su looked somewhat refreshed, but nose tip was shiny, obviously also sweating.

"Any abnormalities?" Niu Dazhuang asked.

Gou Laifu shook his head, tail wagging listlessly. Bao Su didn't speak, just slightly nodded.

Huang Biao looked at them, suddenly feeling irritated. These people, repeating the same work every day, discovering the same abnormalities, reporting the same reports no one cared about. What for? For that hundred lubi? For this pig food?

Lunch came. Still thin porridge, hard buns, stewed cabbage, one egg. Huang Biao collected his portion, didn't find a place to sit, directly leaned against the warehouse outer wall's shadow to eat. He ate very fast, three or two mouthfuls finishing, whole egg swallowed. Then he pulled out a cigarette—this time really gone, cigarette pack completely empty. He irritably crumpled the cigarette pack, throwing it out hard. The paper ball drew an arc in the air, landing in distant dust.

He leaned against the wall, closing his eyes. Sunlight through eyelids, a burning red. The scar on his face hurt more in high temperature, like countless fine needles pricking.

Footsteps. He opened his eyes, it was Gou Laifu.

"Brother Biao," the puppy beastkin said softly, holding half an egg in his hand, "You want it? I can't finish it."

Huang Biao stared at that half egg. Egg white smooth, yolk intact. Gou Laifu's eyes carried a bit of讨好, tail wagging in small arcs.

He closed his eyes, continuing to lean against the wall. Sunlight baked his skin, sweat constantly emerging, then quickly evaporated by the heat, leaving a layer of salt frost on skin, stinging and itchy.

He remembered that night eight years ago. Also this hot, air sticky. That abandoned warehouse in the southern part of the city, darkness, only a few dim yellow lamps. The old big brother patted his shoulder saying: "Biao, it's up to you. After this is done, brother will take you to eat good food and drink good wine."

He believed it. Took a cleaver and went. Result? The other side had more people, brighter knives. The old big brother's voice came from the distance: "Biao, hold on! Brother's going to get people!" Then came the sound of footsteps fading away.

Huang Biao opened his eyes, sunlight making him tear up. He wiped his face with the back of his hand, hand stained with sweat, and a bit of wetness from the corner of his eye. He cursed, straightening his body.

Noon break wasn't over yet, but he didn't want to stay here anymore. He circled around the warehouse, walking to that patch of wasteland behind. Wild grass grew half a person tall, swaying in hot wind. He found a place with deep grass, lying down.

Grass blades pricked his back, soil's breath mixed with humus smell rushing up. He stared at the sky. Sky was very blue, not a cloud, blue to the point of falseness, blue to the point of dazzling.

## Scene-5 The Truck's Dust

Afternoon work was unloading and loading cargo.

A blue truck stopped in the center of the courtyard, driver was a hyena beastkin, face full of impatience, slapping the delivery slip into Gou Laifu's hand like sending off a beggar. Huang Biao leaned against the warehouse doorframe, watching Gou Laifu手忙脚乱地核对单子, Bao Su supervising silently nearby.

"In... inside..." Gou Laifu stammered.

Huang Biao ignored him, directly walking into the warehouse. Niu Dazhuang and Xiong Houshi were already moving cases. Heavy wooden cases, each fifty or sixty jin. When Niu Dazhuang lifted them, back muscles bulged, sweat soaking the green uniform, sticking tightly to skin, outlining thick muscle contours. Xiong Houshi's strength was even greater, carrying two cases at once, but moving slowly, carefully.

Huang Biao didn't help move. He leaned against the shelf, watching them move. Sweat flowed down from his temple, flowing into the scar's grooves, stinging and itchy. He wiped it with the back of his hand, hand stained with sweat and dust.

Cases moved out one by one, stacked beside the truck. Gou Laifu inspected seals, Bao Su supervised loading. Huang Biao watched the driver—that hyena beastkin leaned against the door smoking, gaze alertly sweeping the surroundings, not like an ordinary driver.

Reaching the fifteenth case, the driver suddenly walked over, pointing at one of the cases: "This one, put it down gently!"

Huang Biao looked over. That case was the same as the others, wooden, seal intact. But the driver was especially nervous about this one. Huang Biao narrowed his eyes, walking over, pretending to inspect the seal, hand patting the case surface.

Echo was a bit hollow.

He glanced at the driver. The driver was staring at him, warning in his gaze.

Huang Biao withdrew his hand, stepping back. Didn't speak. He knew the rules—don't look at what you shouldn't, don't ask what you shouldn't, don't touch what you shouldn't.

All loaded, driver signed, jumped into the cab. Truck started, spewing a puff of black smoke, slowly driving out the main gate. Dust flew up, lingering long without dispersing.

Huang Biao stood in the dust, watching the truck disappear in the direction. Then he walked to where that case had just been stacked, crouching down, touching the ground with his hand. On the concrete floor were a few dark traces, like liquid dripping then drying. He scraped a bit, crushed it, brought it to his nose to smell.

Sweet fishiness. Same as what he smelled in Area C in the morning.

He stood up, patting his hands. Gou Laifu and Bao Su had already begun inventorying the newly arrived cargo. Huang Biao didn't go over to help, he walked to the faucet, washing hands. Water was ice-cold, rushing on hands, but couldn't wash away that indescribable irritation in his heart.

Newly arrived cargo was also a pile of cases, seals brand new, several case corners had collision marks. Gou Laifu was recording seriously, Bao Su was inspecting seals. Huang Biao watched from afar, suddenly feeling all this was very ridiculous—they were like a group of blind people, inspecting a pile of cases containing secrets, but forever not knowing what the secrets were.

The sun began to set in the west. Shadows lengthened, front courtyard half in sunlight, half in shadow. Huang Biao walked to the shadow side, leaning against the wall to sit down. Fumbling out the switchblade from his boot, playing with it in his hand. Blade popped out, closed, popped out again. Metal friction sound in the silent courtyard was especially clear.

Gou Laifu finished inventorying, carrying the record board walking over, seeing the knife in Huang Biao's hand, footsteps paused.

"Brother Biao, you..." he said softly.

Huang Biao raised his head, gaze cold: "What?"

Gou Laifu shook his head, carrying the record board and quickly walking away. Huang Biao watched his back, sneering, closing the knife, tucking it back into the boot.

Coward. All cowards.

Including himself.

## Scene-6 Oil Lamp and Old Wounds

Dinner time.

Thin porridge, hard buns, pickled vegetables. Huang Biao ate very fast, almost without chewing swallowing it down. Then he returned to the dormitory, oil lamp was already lit.

The dormitory was filled with oil lamp burning black smoke and male beastkin gathering thick body odor. Huang Biao walked to his own bed, not lying down. He dragged out a broken麻袋 from under the bed, inside was his entire belongings—two changes of old clothes, a worn belt, and a small iron box.

He opened the iron box. Inside weren't many things: several rusty bullet casings, picked up from the streets before; a wrinkled photo, above was a young tiger beastkin, smile brilliant, face without scar—that was Huang Biao from eight years ago, eighteen years old, just joined the streets, still believed in loyalty, still believed in the future. Photo edges were rubbed white from handling, curling.

And a letter. Letter paper was already yellow, handwriting messy, written when he was lying in the black clinic after being injured, using a scavenged pencil stub. Written to that old big brother.

Letter wasn't sent out. Because written to the end, he also didn't know where to send it to. Old big brother had long disappeared, like he never existed.

Huang Biao looked at himself in the photo. Orange-yellow fur bright, eyes had a kind of naive fierceness, not yet the later turbid malice. Face smooth, without that centipede-like scar crawling on it.

He used his finger to stroke that face in the photo. Fingertips rough, scraping against paper surface, rustling.

Then he turned the photo over, back facing up. Out of sight, out of mind.

Oil lamp flame flickered on the wall, casting swaying shadows. Dormitory sounds were noisy—Xiong Houshi was talking about his dream from last night, voice deep; Gou Laifu was looking at his broken book in the corner, page-turning sound very light; Bao Su was wiping shoes, movements meticulous; Niu Dazhuang leaned against the head of the bed, reading a letter by lamplight, expression gentle.

Huang Biao looked at all this, suddenly feeling stuffy in his chest. He stood up, walking to the door, pushing it open and going out.

Night was thick. No moon, only a few stars coldly nailed to the sky. Warehouse's massive outline was silent in the darkness, like a sleeping giant beast.

He walked to the center of the courtyard, looking up at the sky. Sky was deep blue, edges carrying a bit of purple, stars sparse, light weak. Night wind blew over, carrying wild grass and soil's breath, and from the distant town floated a faint, almost imperceptible coal smoke smell.

He fumbled out the switchblade from his boot, weighing it in his hand. Then suddenly stabbed forward with force, movement fierce, carrying the sound of breaking wind. Then second knife, third knife—all street fighting wild ways, no pattern, but fast enough, fierce enough.

Stab, slash, thrust, chop. Movements became faster and faster, breathing became increasingly rough. Sweat seeped from forehead, flowing into eyes, stinging painfully. But he didn't stop, continuing to wave that short knife, like fighting with some invisible enemy.

Until panting heavily, arms sore, he stopped. Sweat soaked the green uniform, sticking tightly to skin, ice-cold. The scar on his face burned like fire after intense exercise, like just being chopped.

He panted, staring at the deep darkness. There was nothing there, only night wind blowing through wild grass's rustling sounds.

"Old bastard..." he cursed in a low voice, don't know who he was cursing. Maybe that old big brother who abandoned him, maybe the opponent who chopped him, maybe this dog-day life, maybe... himself.

He closed the knife, tucking it back into the boot. Then slowly walked back to the dormitory.

Oil lamp was still lit, but quite a few people were already asleep. Snores rose and fell, teeth grinding sounds, sleep-talking sounds. Huang Biao walked to his own bed, took off boots, lay down.

Bed board hard, pressing against those old injuries on his back. He closed his eyes, but his mind was unusually clear. Dripping sound, sweet fishiness, brand new seals, driver's alert gaze, hollow wooden case... these details emerged in the darkness, piecing together some blurry outline.

He knew this warehouse wasn't clean. Knew Beastshield was doing shady dealings. Knew they these bottom-level杂兵, were just chess pieces that could be abandoned at any time.

But what could he do? Report? Report to whom? Beastshield itself was the rule. Run? Run where? This scar on his face, like a brand, wherever he went people would recognize—oh, that's Huang Biao, used to mix in the southern part of the city, got chopped by someone, chickened out, ran.

No way out.

He turned over, facing the wall. Where wall plaster peeled away, revealing the yellow mud and straw mixed filler inside. He stared at that rough wall surface, for a very long time.

Then, before sinking into sleep, his last thought was: tomorrow, another same day. Patrol, move cargo, eat pig food, receive that hundred lubi. Cycle after cycle, until one day, maybe in some dark corner, die quietly like a wild dog.

This was his way of living.

He accepted it.
