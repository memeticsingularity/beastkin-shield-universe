# Bao Su's Day

## Scene-1 The Texture of Darkness

Darkness had layers.

Bao Su woke in the third layer of darkness—the first layer was the pure black curtain after closing eyes, the second was the iron-gray diluted black seeping through the dormitory windows sealed with burlap sacks, the third was the black filtered through his own eyelids, carrying the warmth and sound of blood flow. He didn't move, first listening. This was a habit developed over years: before confirming safety, the body maintained absolute stillness.

Sounds seeped in from every corner of the darkness, like water seeping through stone slabs.

From the lower bunk on the left came Niu Dazhuang's heavy, even breathing, a slight tremor in his nasal cavity with each inhale, like an old bellows. On the upper right was Huang Biao, sleeping restlessly, turning over with the iron bed frame emitting sharp friction sounds, followed by indistinct sleep-talking, tone carrying malice. Diagonally across, Xiong Houshi's snores were like a steam engine with a broken valve, rising and falling, carrying the gurgling sound of phlegm rolling deep in his chest. Gou Laifu was grinding his teeth, fine crunching sounds, frequency very fast, a canine's nervous twitch during sleep. Xiong Dali was talking in his sleep, muttering "Ma... cake...", voice honest and fuzzy.

There were more sounds: rats scratching at holes in the wall corners, distant warehouse tin roof being moved by night wind making wailing sounds, even more distant wild dogs' intermittent barking. Bao Su's ears turned slightly, short dense black fur on the ear pinnae capturing the vibrations of sound waves in the air. He classified and filed these sounds: harmless, normal, needing attention.

Then he opened his eyes.

The dormitory was still dark, but he could see. Feline beastkin pupils dilated in dark places to nearly fill the entire eye socket, collecting every trace of faint light. He saw the yellow water stains on the ceiling from rain leaks, shaped like a twisted beast face. He saw the green uniforms hanging on the opposite bunk, like hanged corpses in the darkness. He saw Gou Laifu's ankle exposed outside the blanket, the edge of the white sock already gray.

He didn't get up immediately, but first checked his body's condition. This wasn't a conscious action, but a scan etched into his bones: muscle relaxation—good, no excessive fatigue; joint mobility—cervical spine slightly stiff, left from yesterday's improper posture while moving cargo; heartbeat—fifty-two per minute, steady and powerful; breathing—deep and slow, diaphragm moving fully. Then there were those old injuries: the seventeen-centimeter knife scar below the left scapula, now only slightly sore; the right lower leg fibula once fractured, hurting on rainy days, fine today; four old rib fractures, healing not quite perfect, feeling slight misalignment during deep breaths.

These injuries were his map, recording another period of life. He never recalled those days, but his body remembered.

He slowly sat up, movement smooth like liquid pouring from a container. The iron bed frame made no sound—he knew where force would make it creak, where it wouldn't. His weight was around one hundred seventy jin, for a black panther beastkin this was a lean, strong build, without the thick fat layer of cattle beastkin, nor the exaggerated muscle blocks of bear beastkin, but rather that kind of tightly woven, clearly lined muscle, covering the slender skeleton, like a carefully forged suit of armor.

Feet probing down, touching the concrete floor. Cold shot up from the soles, climbing along the ankle bones upward. His soles were narrower than most beastkin, high arches, slender toes, able to grip flexibly like hands. This was a feline characteristic, a remnant from tree-dwelling ancestors. He first hooked his toes to his boots—not the issued liberation shoes, but his own black combat boots he'd brought, high tops, deep-treaded soles, suitable for silent movement. Inside the boot was a hidden pocket, containing his personal items: a multi-function tactical folding knife, a whetstone, a small roll of fishing line, and a faded photo, the person on it already unrecognizable.

He put on the boots, not tying the laces. Laces were a hazard—could snag on things, could come loose, could be used by an opponent. He used Velcro and internal straps, three seconds to put on or take off. Then he began dressing.

The green uniform was laid out beside the bed, folded before sleep last night. The fabric was rough, starched, stiff, but in the darkness it had a familiar texture when touched. He put on pants first, movements crisp and decisive. When pulling pants to his waist, he could feel the fabric rubbing against the fur on the outer thighs—black short hair,紧贴 skin, like a layer of velvet. Then the upper garment, pull-over, zipper from chest to lower abdomen. The zipper was metal, ice-cold, sliding across skin raising a layer of goosebumps. He pulled the zipper closed, adjusted collar, cuffs, hem. Every movement was precise, no excess.

Finally socks and gloves. Socks were standard issue white long socks, cotton, already washed thin, the bottom printed with his number: G-536. He put on the socks, sock tops at two finger-widths below the knee, the elastic band already lost elasticity, needing to be pulled up. Gloves were fingerless, palm had anti-slip rubber granules, finger parts worn shiny.

Fully dressed, he sat on the bed edge, beginning morning stretches. This was his ritual, every day like this. First neck, slowly turning left and right, bones making fine cracking sounds. Then shoulder joints, arms drawing circles, scapulae sliding beneath back skin, feeling those old scar tissues underneath being slowly stretched apart. Then spine, bending forward, unfolding vertebra by vertebra, then leaning back, thoracic vertebrae making more obvious sounds. Finally lower limbs, hip joints, knee joints, ankle joints. Each movement was slow, full, carrying some kind of devout focus.

The stretches lasted seven minutes. When finished, his body had completely transitioned from sleep state to standby state—muscles warm, joints lubricated, cardiopulmonary function elevated to twenty percent above basal metabolic rate. He exhaled, white mist briefly lingering in the cold air before dissipating.

At this time, Niu Dazhuang woke.

Bao Su didn't turn his head, but his ears captured that familiar sound sequence: first the change in breathing rhythm, from deep to shallow and rapid; then a light cough in the throat, carrying phlegm sound; followed by the sound of hand rubbing on rough bedsheets; finally that characteristic groan of the iron bed frame—three parts to the left, bed leg rusted area; five parts to the right, beam bent area.

Niu Dazhuang sat up, movements slow and heavy, like a water buffalo just crawled out of a mud swamp. Bao Su could smell his scent: sweat, fodder, and the faint fishy-muttony odor unique to cattle, mixed together, especially obvious in the morning cold air. Then came the sounds of dressing, fabric rubbing, belt buckle clinking, shoes dragging on the ground.

The dormitory came alive.

Bao Su stood up, walking to the door. The light cord hung there, a dirty hemp rope. He didn't pull it—feline beastkin disliked sudden bright light, it would burn dilated pupils. He bypassed empty cans, stinky socks, unknown buttons dropped on the floor, walking to his own washing spot.

His washbasin was the cleanest in the dormitory. Enamel, white, with a simple blue flower printed on the bottom, edges without chips. Toothbrush standing upright in the cup, bristles facing up, toothpaste lying horizontally across the cup mouth, cap screwed tight. Towel folded into a square, placed on the basin edge. Everything had order.

He picked up the basin, walking to the faucet. Someone was already queuing, Gou Laifu first in line, standing on tiptoe reaching for the faucet. Bao Su silently stood at the end of the line. While waiting, he observed the corridor. Concrete floor damp, puddled with water splashed from last night's washing, water stains reflecting faint light. Lower half of walls painted green, upper half rough concrete, wall plaster peeling in several places, revealing the yellow mud and straw mixed filler inside. The fluorescent tube on the ceiling blackened at both ends, a sign of approaching end of life.

Gou Laifu's turn. The puppy beastkin collected half a basin of water, crouching in the corner to brush teeth, movements clumsy, toothpaste foam splashing everywhere. Huang Biao cut in line, no one dared to speak, after washing he even flicked water onto the people behind, grinning, the scar on his face twisting.

Finally Bao Su's turn. He collected half a basin of water, temperature ice-cold and bone-piercing. He brushed teeth first, toothbrush moving slowly across tooth surfaces, every tooth brushed, gums, tongue surface, upper palate. Mint-flavored cheap toothpaste dissolved in mouth, carrying chemical fragrance's pungency. He rinsed, spat out water mixed with blood streaks—gums had always been not great. Then washed face, water patting on face, cold stimulation making skin tighten. He washed very carefully, forehead, eye sockets, both sides of nose, chin, behind ears, neck. Every place washed, washing away overnight accumulated grease and dust.

Finished washing, he splashed water against the wall base, raising a small cloud of dust. Then pulled out a small piece of soap from his pants pocket—not scavenged from the cafeteria, but bought himself, unscented, weak alkaline. He washed hands, palms, backs, between fingers, nail crevices. Soap foam was fine, rubbing between palms making slight rustling sounds. Finished washing, he shook his hands, not using a towel to dry, letting nature air-dry.

At this time, Niu Dazhuang walked over, carrying lunch boxes. Six aluminum lunch boxes strung on hemp rope, clinking in his thick hands.

"Whose turn to get food today?" Gou Laifu asked, ears perked up.

"Me," Bao Su said.

Niu Dazhuang nodded, handing over the lunch box string. Hemp rope pressed into palm, rough, carrying Niu Dazhuang's palm temperature. The lunch boxes were warm, probably just taken out from the cafeteria. Bao Su picked them up, turning to walk out.

At the end of the corridor was the door, pushing it open, morning air rushed in.

## Scene-2 Morning Cold Light

Outside was colder than inside.

When Bao Su stepped out of the dormitory building, morning cold air was like a layer of invisible ice water, instantly wrapping around him. He could feel the skin beneath the green uniform fabric quickly raising a layer of goosebumps, black short fur slightly standing up, trying to retain body heat. He took a breath, air clean, carrying dew, soil, and distant field smells, completely different from that turbid body odor inside the dormitory.

The sky wasn't fully bright yet, on the eastern horizon there was a hint of fish-belly white, slowly rendering outward, from iron-gray to gray-blue, then to pale cyan. Stars hadn't completely faded, a few of the brightest still stubbornly nailed to the deep blue sky curtain, light weak but firm. The moon was in the west, only a shallow silver hook left, thin as a piece of ice.

The warehouse was fifty meters ahead, its massive outline in the dawn light like a crouching giant beast. The tin roof reflected sky light, presenting a cold metallic texture. The barbed wire on the walls was pitch black, spikes pointing skyward, in the gradually brightening sky like some giant beast's teeth.

Bao Su carried the lunch boxes, walking along the concrete road. His footsteps were very light, sole contacting ground with almost no sound. This was the result of years of training: ball of foot landing first, weight slowly transferring, toes gripping, then heel lightly setting down. Each step was like a cat stepping on fallen leaves, only extremely fine rustling sounds.

There were traces of dew wetting on the road, concrete surface dark and light patches. His boots stepped on them, leaving shallow wet prints, but quickly evaporated by his own body temperature and walking wind. He could feel the temperature changes transmitted from the soles: dry places ice-cold, wet places even colder.

Reaching the warehouse main gate, he stopped, looking up. The gate was two heavy iron plates, painted dark green, but paint peeling very badly, revealing the rust-red base underneath. Light seeped through the door crack—night shift people hadn't left yet, lights on inside.

He pushed open the small door beside it, door hinge making a dry creaking sound. The sound traveled far in the morning silence.

Inside was the warehouse front hall, a dim yellow lamp hanging from the roof, light barely illuminating this not-large space. ## Scene-3 The Stomach of Darkness

The feeling of pushing open the warehouse main gate was like pushing open a giant beast's mouth.

Darkness surged out, not that pure,虚无 black, but black with thickness, weight, temperature. It wrapped in familiar smells rushing at his face: dust, rust, wood decay's sweet fishiness, machine oil, chemicals, and some deeper layer, indescribable, like countless secrets fermented together.

Bao Su stepped in, darkness instantly swallowing him.

But he could see. Pupils dilated to the extreme in an instant, collecting the dim yellow light from those few safety lights in the distance overhead. Light was weak, barely outlining the massive, silent contours of the cargo piles. Up close, flashlight had to be turned on—not to illuminate the path ahead, but to check details. He flicked on the flashlight, beam stabbing into darkness, like a dull knife cutting through thick liquid.

Gou Laifu followed one step behind him, breathing somewhat rapid, a sign of nervousness. Canine sense of smell functioned in the darkness, Bao Su could hear his nose twitching sounds, like distinguishing paths in a scent maze.

"Same as usual?" Gou Laifu asked softly, voice sounding very light in the emptiness.

Bao Su nodded, not speaking. The two walked one after another into the alleyways formed by the cargo piles.

Area A was all miscellaneous goods. Cases varied in size and material, from wooden cases to cardboard boxes to metal cases, stacked haphazardly to the ceiling, like a group of crouching, strangely shaped giant beasts in the shadows. Alleyways were narrow, only allowing one person through, two people had to go one after another.

Bao Su took the lead, flashlight beam sweeping left and right. Beam swept across case surfaces, illuminating labels, numbers, seals on them. His eyes quickly scanned this information, brain automatically comparing with memory: this case was here yesterday, seal at this angle, securing rope tied in this knot. Any subtle change would be captured.

He inspected very carefully, but not with eyes, more with hands and nose. He would stop, use fingertips to lightly touch seal edges, feeling paper texture, glue bonding flatness, stamp凹凸 texture. Seals were Beastshield special paper, stamped with red seal, once torn and re-pasted, glue's stickiness and texture would have subtle differences. His fingertips could distinguish these differences.

Nose was also working. He inhaled deeply, letting scent molecules decompose in his nasal cavity. Aged dust's dry astringency, damp wood's faintly sweet mustiness, rust's metallic fishiness, these were background. Above these, he searched for abnormalities: chemical sting, organic decay, blood's sweet fishiness, or other smells that shouldn't appear here.

Reaching a pile of wooden crates, he stopped.

"Wait," he said in a low voice, voice almost inaudible in the darkness, but Gou Laifu immediately halted.

Bao Su crouched down, flashlight beam focusing on the bottom of a wooden crate. There was a palm-sized dark stain there, color darker than surroundings, edges irregular. He leaned in to smell. Dust smell, pine wood smell, and... an extremely faint, sweet-fishy rust smell. No, not rust. It was blood. Dried blood.

He used his index fingernail to lightly scrape that stained area. Surface somewhat sticky, scraping off a bit of dark red powder. Powder was very fine, having a slippery feel when pinched apart on fingertip.

"Record it," he said.

Gou Laifu pulled out the record board from his back pocket, using a short pencil to write crookedly: Area A-9, bottom wooden crate, dark red stain on case bottom, sticky to touch, suspected dried liquid.

Finished writing, he looked up at Bao Su: "Brother Su, should we report it?"

"Record first," Bao Su stood up, patting dust from his hands, "Wait until patrol's done, report together."

This was the rule. Can't run to find the foreman making a fuss upon discovering a little abnormality, that way you'd be scolded as "troublesome," "trying to slack off." Save up, gather a few, report once, showing you work seriously, without being too annoying. As for whether anyone cares after reporting, that's not their concern.

The two continued forward. Alleyways twisted and turned, like a maze. Flashlight beam cut a trembling path of light in the darkness, beyond which was blackness too thick to dissolve. Bao Su's back began to slightly sweat. Not from tiredness, but that physiological reaction from being constantly in a tense state. Green uniform inner lining rubbed against skin, sweat-wet places ice-cold, unwet places stuffy and hot. He could feel his heart beating steadily in his chest, thump, thump, thump, rhythm mixing with footsteps.

His ears remained upright, capturing every sound beyond their footsteps. In the distance was a dripping sound, very regular, probably a leaking pipe somewhere. Further away, there was an extremely faint, metallic creaking sound, but indistinct. There were also rats scurrying deep in the cargo piles, claws scratching wooden boards' fine sounds.

Those strange seals, those odors that shouldn't be there, those trucks coming and going late at night, those drivers with nervous expressions.

Very light, like something scraping across a metal surface, or like a small pebble rolling down.

Bao Su didn't speak. He listened for a while longer, confirming no follow-up sounds, then continued forward. But footsteps became lighter, each step like stepping on cotton. Ears fully open, collecting three-hundred-sixty-degree sound field information.

For the rest of the patrol, both were more alert. Flashlight beam swept slower, inspection more careful. But no more abnormalities discovered. Only darkness, endless darkness, and those silent cases.

Reaching the end of Area A, light appeared ahead—it was the passage to Area B, where there was a safety light. Dim yellow light seeped over from there, casting a fuzzy halo on the ground.

Bao Su turned off the flashlight. Pupils contracted in an instant, adapting to the slightly brighter light. He glanced at the record board, three abnormalities already recorded: blood stain, sweet metal case, the strange sound from just now.

"Almost done," he said, "Let's head back."

Gou Laifu nodded, obviously relieved. The two turned around, walking back along the way they came. The return journey felt shorter than the way there, perhaps because of familiarity, perhaps because knowing the exit was ahead.

Reaching the warehouse main gate, morning sunlight leaked in through the door crack, forming a dazzling band of light. Bao Su pushed the door open, light instantly flooding in, like a tide drowning them.

Outside, the sky was already fully bright.
